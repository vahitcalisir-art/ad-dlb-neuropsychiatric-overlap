## Bibliographic record
Wu Y, Sun Z, Zheng Q, et al.; 2024; Pervasive biases in proxy genome-wide association studies based on parental history of Alzheimer's disease; Nature Genetics 56(12):2696–2703; DOI 10.1038/s41588-024-01963-9 (PMID 39496879; PMC11929606; NIH author manuscript; earlier bioRxiv preprint 10.1101/2023.10.13.562272); methodological genetic-epidemiology study (UK Biobank GWAX, GWAS-by-subtraction with GSUB, genetic correlation, PRS, MR, bias-reduction benchmarking); access route PMC-XML (published version, author manuscript)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: Almost every recent AD GWAS meta-analyses clinically diagnosed case–control studies with proxy (parental-history) studies. Uncorrected survival bias and nonrandom participation in parental illness surveys cause substantial GWAS–GWAX discrepancies; the current AD GWAX give highly misleading genetic correlations between AD risk and higher education, affecting downstream genetic-epidemiological applications; calls for caution with middle-aged biobank cohorts and proxy-reported parental history. (Published wording "the current AD GWAX provide ... genetic epidemiological applications" differs slightly from the preprint "current AD GWAX ... genetic epidemiologic applications".)
- Introduction: GWAX (Liu et al.) uses biobank participants' reports of parental diagnoses; every recent AD GWAS meta-analysed clinical case–control GWAS with GWAX to boost power; the largest AD GWAS to date (Bellenguez 2022, ref 9) did not share separate GWAS and GWAX results, only meta-analysed results. Earlier critiques focused on measurement error/heritability; infinitesimal biases may not show at top loci but can severely bias genetic correlation and PRS. Introduces GSUB (GWAS-by-subtraction) to quantify bias sources (survival bias; participation and reporting biases). (Published wording "did not share separate association results" replaces the preprint "has stopped the earlier convention of sharing separate association results".)
- Methods: UKB European-ancestry GWAX with Regenie v3.2.2 (covariates sex, year of birth, array); parental AD/dementia from data fields 20107/20110; proxy cases if either parent affected, controls if neither; "do not know"/prefer not to answer removed; adopted excluded; 47,993 proxy cases and 315,096 controls. Parental illness awareness GWAS in UKB (59,471 …"do not know" as controls (47,993/349,165); METAL vs GenomicSEM meta-analysis.
- Results: GWAX replicated Kunkle loci: effect correlation 0.97 (APOE excluded), slope 0.63, 1.15 (s.e. 0.20) after winner's-curse correction. Across 40 traits, AD GWAS vs GWAX rg = 0.63 (P = 3.9 × 10−31) but divergent correlations: total cholesterol (0.13) and hippocampal volume (−0.23) significant only with GWAS; ADHD (−0.16 vs 0.05) and CAD (−0.31 vs −0.1) more strongly correlated with lower AD risk in GWAX; APOE exclusion did not change this. Seven traits significant in both (FDR 0.05), three flipped sign; EA rg −0.13 (GWAS, P = 2.4 × 10−5) vs +0.17 (GWAX, P = 1.7 × 10−11). Ten AD studies 2013–2022: case–control negative, family-history positive, meta-analyses in between; Bellenguez 2022 rg with EA 0.03 (P = 0.18). HRS PRS: GWAS −0.05 (P = 2.5 × 10−11), GWAX −0.0017 (P = 0.80), Bellenguez −0.03 (P = 1.2 × 10−7, attenuated despite larger N). MR EA→AD: protective with GWAS (−0.36, P = 8.6 × 10−3), positive non-significant with GWAX and with Bellenguez meta-analysis. Non-AD (bias) component vs 50 traits (16 FDR-significant, Fig. 4): positive with EA 0.26, parental (indirect) EA effect 0.53, cognition 0.19, non-cognitive EA 0.23; NEGATIVE with MDD (−0.11, P = 0.012), schizophrenia (−0.13, P = 8.3 × 10−3), CAD (−0.14), ADHD (−0.17), epilepsy (−0.20), heart failure (−0.24) — interpreted as survival bias (parents must live long enough to be diagnosed; competing risks), supported by younger age distribution of proxy controls and their parents. Four non-AD dementias (PD, ALS, FTD, Lewy body dementia) null rg near zero. Survey participation rg 0.44 (P = 1.1 × 10−9), not knowing parental illness −0.18. Bias reduction: Marioni null EA rg and CAD rg reduced (−0.081); Jansen flips EA rg 0.17 → −0.15 but gives positive CAD rg (0.14); weighting did not help; participation and "do not know" subtraction reduced but did not remove bias. Meta-analysis with GWAS yields rg between GWAX and GWAS; METAL and GenomicSEM comparable; meta-analysis alone cannot remove the bias. (Published wording of several sentences differs from the preprint; numbers unchanged — see Claims.)
- Discussion: Biases present in almost all published AD GWAS including proxy cases; education central. Three mechanisms: (1) survival bias — without adjustment, spurious negative genetic correlations between AD GWAX and other health outcomes (variants protective for other diseases appear to raise AD risk through longevity); (2) awareness/participation (health awareness, parent relationships, SES); (3) non-AD dementia in parental reports (no evidence, possibly low power of non-AD dementia GWAS). Biases not mitigated by simple meta-analysis with case–control GWAS. LT-FH also gives positive EA rg. Criticises practices in Bellenguez-type studies (refs 7, 9): combining clinical and proxy cases in logistic regression without scaling effect sizes; using both sibling and parental proxies (extra survival bias). Recommends releasing GWAS and GWAX summary statistics separately.
- Limitations stated by authors: case–control GWAS treated as gold standard but Kunkle–CAD negative rg suggests uncorrected survival bias in clinical GWAS too; FinnGen late-onset AD GWAS considered but ICD-derived AD status may be inaccurate and controls not age-matched (may exacerbate survival bias), so not a gold standard; benchmark metrics (EA, CAD rg) uncertain; UKB non-representativeness; European descent only.
- Supplementary material: not available (Supplementary Tables 1–21 and Supplementary Figs referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Large, carefully designed methodological study (UKB GWAX ~363k; AllofUs WGS; HRS n = 12,018) with multiple bias-reduction benchmarks, GNOVA with overlap correction, simulation-validated GSUB. Peer-reviewed (Nature Genetics 2024); text is the NIH author manuscript of the published article. Limitations: rg-based benchmarks indirect; psychiatric-trait evidence comes from the non-AD component (MDD −0.11, SCZ −0.13, nominal P ~0.01, within the 16 FDR-significant traits); direct AD-GWAX vs psychiatric rg for each trait is only in Supplementary Tables. European descent only. No competing interests declared. Consistent with Escott-Price & Hardy and Grotzinger et al. critiques; the FinnGen statement is a caution for our FinnGen R13 sensitivity analysis.

## Relevance to this manuscript
- D2 (central): direction of proxy bias. Survival bias makes AD GWAX — and GWAS–GWAX meta-analyses such as Bellenguez 2022 (our primary AD GWAS), whose rg fall between GWAS and GWAX — show spuriously more NEGATIVE genetic correlations with other health outcomes. The non-AD component correlates negatively with MDD (−0.11, P = 0.012) and schizophrenia (−0.13, P = 8.3 × 10−3) (also ADHD −0.17, CAD −0.14, epilepsy, heart failure), so psychiatric rg with proxy-containing AD are expected to be biased downward (toward lower AD risk / null or negative). EA/cognition rg pushed positive. Meta-analysis cannot remove the bias. Bellenguez practice (unscaled clinical+proxy logistic regression; sibling+parental proxies) explicitly criticised; Bellenguez released only meta-analysed results.
- D1: explains discrepancies between prior rg/MR studies using clinical (Kunkle/IGAP) vs proxy-containing AD GWAS (e.g. MR of education).
- D3/D6: FinnGen ICD-based AD with non-age-matched controls may exacerbate survival bias — caveat for our FinnGen R13 clinical AD sensitivity; survival bias even in clinical GWAS; UKB participation bias.
- D4: Lewy body dementia GWAS (ref 30, Chia 2021) showed null rg with the bias component.

## Decision
KEEP — the key D2 source on direction of proxy-AD bias, now read in its peer-reviewed published version (Nat Genet 2024).
