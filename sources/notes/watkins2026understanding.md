## Bibliographic record
Watkins MM, Xhafkollari G, Zhao N, et al.; 2026; Understanding the genetic imperfections of Lewy body dementia; Molecular Neurodegeneration 21:63; DOI 10.1186/s13024-026-00961-1; narrative review; access route PMC-XML (PMC13637220)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: LBD clinically defined by fluctuations, visual hallucinations, RBD, motor dysfunction; co-pathology and overlap with AD and PD hinder diagnosis; SNCA from familial studies; GWAS confirmed SNCA, APOE, GBA; APOE e4 also LBD risk; GBA linked to severe cognitive decline and earlier onset; genetic architecture incomplete, requiring larger, more diverse cohorts and improved phenotyping.
- Introduction: LBD ~10-15% of dementia cases; underdiagnosed (clinical vs neuropathological prevalence gap); DLB and PDD overlap (timing rule); many LBD have coexisting AD pathology; LBD genetic architecture less defined than AD/PD due to small sample sizes, diagnostic variability, limited diversity, overlapping pathology.
- Methods: not applicable (narrative review, no search strategy reported).
- Results (narrative sections): Clinical: key symptoms visual hallucinations, fluctuations, RBD; other symptoms include mood disorders, depression, anxiety, apathy; DLB incidence 0.5-1.6 per 1000 person-years; onset 70-85 years. Pathology: 70-80% of Lewy body disease cases have >=1 co-pathology, most commonly AD. Genetics: Chia 2021 GWAS confirmed SNCA, APOE, GBA and identified BIN1, TMEM175 (Table 1); MAPT and TREM2 not associated; earlier LBD GWAS 1,743 cases (1,324 neuropathological) vs 4,454 controls; another 828 clinically diagnosed DLB vs 82,035 controls. APOE e4 OR 2.46 (2.22-2.74), allele frequency 10% controls vs 21.3% cases; top GWAS hit at APOE; debate whether e4 acts via AD co-pathology or directly on alpha-synuclein (conflicting autopsy studies; animal/iPSC support direct role). APOE not a PD GWAS risk factor. GBA: up to 15-20% carriers in Ashkenazi Jewish LBD; carriers have earlier onset, more visual hallucinations and RBD, less AD pathology.
- Discussion ("imperfections"): no single gene explains most LBD; latest LBD GWAS 2,591 patients vs >60,000 for AD and PD; cohort building hampered by clinical heterogeneity and co-pathology; misdiagnosis frequent; need for long-read sequencing, fine-mapping (coloc/eQTL/TWAS), PRS limited, multi-ancestry studies.
- Limitations stated by authors: not formally stated as a review; field-level limitations: small samples, diagnostic variability, limited population diversity, overlapping pathology.
- Supplementary material: not available (none)

## Quality appraisal
Narrative review by an established LBD genetics group (co-author on Chia 2021); peer-reviewed; no systematic search. Restates primary GWAS (Chia 2021, Guerreiro 2018), but Chia 2021 itself is not in this batch, and the explicit sample-size comparison is a useful synthesis. No competing interests. Consistent with mckeith2017diagnosis on SNCA/GBA/APOE.

## Relevance to this manuscript
D4: best source in this batch for DLB genetics (SNCA, APOE, GBA, BIN1, TMEM175), APOE e4 effect size in LBD, the AD-pathology-mediation debate for APOE, and the DLB GWAS sample-size limitation (2,591 vs >60,000). I3: DLB rarely studied genetically, APOE dominance; supports the rationale for local rg and APOE-excluded MR. D6: diagnostic misclassification and co-pathology in DLB case definitions; limited ancestral diversity. D4 clinical: lists depression, anxiety, apathy and visual hallucinations among LBD symptoms.

## Decision
KEEP — directly supports the DLB genetics and GWAS power limitation arguments (D4, I3, D6); Chia 2021 should still be cited as the primary GWAS source.
