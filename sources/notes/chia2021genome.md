## Bibliographic record
Chia R, Sabir MS, Bandres-Ciga S, et al.; 2021; Genome sequencing analysis identifies new loci associated with Lewy body dementia and provides insights into its genetic architecture; Nature Genetics 53(3):294–303; DOI 10.1038/s41588-021-00785-3 (PMID 33589841; PMC7946812; NIH author manuscript); whole-genome sequencing case–control GWAS with rare-variant, heritability, colocalization, genetic risk score and pathway analyses; access route PMC-XML (published version, author manuscript)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: WGS in large cohorts of LBD cases and neurologically healthy controls to study the genetic architecture of this understudied dementia and create a resource; GWAS identified five independent risk loci; gene-aggregation tests implicated GBA; genetic risk scores show LBD shares risk profiles and pathways with AD and PD.
- Introduction: LBD is clinically heterogeneous, characterised by progressive cognitive decline, parkinsonism and visual hallucinations; no disease-modifying therapy; Lewy bodies/neurites as in PD, and most patients have AD co-pathology, leading to the unproven PD–AD continuum hypothesis; ~1.4 million prevalent US cases; WGS in 2,981 LBD patients and 4,391 healthy individuals; five loci replicated in an independent cohort.
- Methods: 5,154 European-ancestry participants (2,981 LBD cases, 2,173 controls) from 17 European and 27 North American sites/consortia, plus convenience controls (Wellderly n = 1,202; NIA/AMP-PD n = 1,016) = 4,391 controls; controls without cognitive decline or neurological deficits; 605 pathologically confirmed controls. Cases: pathologically definite or clinically probable LBD per consensus criteria; 1,789 (69.0%) autopsy-confirmed and 802 (31.0%) clinically probable; 63.4% male. Illumina HiSeq X Ten, CCDG functional-equivalence pipeline, GRCh38DH alignment, GATK 2016 Best Practices. QC: removed contamination, heterozygosity, low call rate, sex discordance, duplicates, non-European ancestry (PCA vs HapMap 3), relatedness (pi-hat > 0.125); variant filters incl. Wellderly/TOPMed/gnomAD frequency checks; non-autosomal variants excluded; 6,651 samples after QC. GWAS: logistic regression in PLINK 2.0, MAF > 1%, covariates sex, age, PC1/3/4/5/7; λ1000 = 1.004; GWS P < 5.0 × 10−8; analysed n = 2,591 cases / 4,027 controls. Replication: two independent non-overlapping European-ancestry array cohorts (970 cases / 8,928 controls), TOPMed imputation, METAL fixed effects. Heritability: GREML-LDMS (GCTA 1.26.0), unrelated individuals, MAF > 1%, adjusted for sex, age, 5 PCs and a disease prevalence of 0.1% for ascertainment. SKAT-O (MAF ≤ 1%, MAC ≥ 3), Bonferroni 2.86 × 10−6 (0.05/17,483 genes) — preprint reported 2.52 × 10−6. Coloc v4.0.1 (eQTLGen n = 31,684; PsychENCODE n = 1,387; p12 = 5 × 10−6; PPH4 ≥ 0.90). AD and PD genetic risk scores in PLINK 1.9; PRSice-2 LBD PRS for g:Profiler pathways; CDR association in 214 NACC cases. Summary statistics in GWAS Catalog, converted hg38 → hg19 with LiftOver; individual data dbGaP phs001963.v1.p1.
- Results: Five GWS loci (Table 1, hg38; discovery OR, P): GBA rs2230288 2.89, 1.28 × 10−12; BIN1 rs6733839 1.25, 4.16 × 10−9 (novel); TMEM175 rs6599388 1.25, 3.54 × 10−8 (novel); SNCA-AS1 rs7680557 0.79, 9.73 × 10−11; APOE rs769449 2.46, 4.65 × 10−63; all replicated (meta P down to 2.57 × 10−101 for APOE). BIN1 risk allele associated with Braak tangle stage (P = 0.0002); second APOE signal; same loci in pathologically defined subanalysis (n = 1,789 cases). Loci explain ~1% of narrow-sense h2 = 10.81% (95% CI 8.28%–13.32%, P = 9.17 × 10−4). SKAT-O: GBA. Coloc: TMEM175 blood eQTL PPH4 = 0.99; SNCA-AS1 brain eQTL PPH4 = 0.96 (increased SNCA-AS1 expression linked to reduced risk, rho −0.42). AD GRS OR 1.66 per SD (1.58–1.74), after APOE adjustment 1.53; PD GRS OR 1.20 (1.14–1.26), after GBA/SNCA/LRRK2 adjustment 1.26; AD GRS associated with earlier death (β −1.77 y) and shorter duration (β −0.90 y); PD GRS with earlier onset (β −0.98); no AD × PD GRS interaction (OR 0.99, P = 0.59). Pathways: amyloid-beta formation, endocytosis, tau protein binding. Highest LBD PRS quintile had more severe baseline CDR impairment (P = 0.009). No LDSC genetic correlation and no psychiatric traits analysed.
- Discussion: LBD architecture overlaps AD (APOE, BIN1 — endosomal trafficking, amyloid, tau propagation) and PD (GBA, TMEM175 — lysosomal; SNCA); SNCA-AS1 signal differs from the PD 3′-end SNCA signal; AD and PD risk scores predict LBD independently even after removing APOE, GBA, SNCA, LRRK2; no AD–PD synergy (larger cohorts needed). Resource: largest LBD WGS repository; nearly 2,000 aged controls; more than two-thirds of cases pathologically confirmed.
- Limitations stated by authors: European ancestry only; short-read sequencing limits complex regions; most participants lacked in-depth standardized phenotype information (new relative to the preprint wording); limited power to detect common variants of small effect despite large sample; missing heritability.
- Supplementary material: not available (Supplementary Tables 1–5 and Note referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Largest LBD genetic study at publication but modest for GWAS (2,591 cases / 4,027 controls); authors acknowledge limited power for small-effect common variants, implying imprecise LDSC h2/rg and few MR instruments for DLB as exposure. Strengths: >2/3 autopsy-confirmed cases, uniform WGS pipeline, independent replication, minimal inflation (λ1000 = 1.004). Bias risks: convenience controls (Wellderly, AMP-PD) handled with frequency-based variant QC; mixed clinical/pathological case definition; LBD umbrella (DLB criteria, PDD not separated in main text); limited standardized phenotyping; European only. Liability h2 assumes prevalence 0.1% (our manuscript assumes DLB 0.005 — not supported by this source). Peer-reviewed (Nature Genetics). Many authors declare industry consultancies. Not MR.

## Relevance to this manuscript
- M1: primary description of the DLB GWAS data source (sample sizes, European ancestry, clinical vs autopsy-confirmed diagnosis, cohort composition, GRCh38 build with hg19 liftover of summary statistics, GWAS Catalog availability).
- D4: DLB clinical features (hallucinations, parkinsonism, AD co-pathology), genetics (GBA, APOE, SNCA-AS1, BIN1, TMEM175), explicit limited power.
- D5: loci/pathways linking DLB to AD and PD (amyloid, tau, endocytosis, lysosome).
- D6: European-only, small sample, weak instruments, limited phenotyping.
- I3: APOE dominant signal (P = 4.65 × 10−63); no psychiatric-trait rg tested.
- M3: does NOT provide a DLB population prevalence for our 0.005 assumption (uses 0.1% for ascertainment adjustment only).

## Decision
KEEP — the DLB GWAS summary-statistics source actually used in the study; now read in the peer-reviewed Nature Genetics version.
