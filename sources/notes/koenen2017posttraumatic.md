## Bibliographic record
Koenen KC, Ratanatharathorn A, Ng L, et al. (on behalf of the WHO World Mental Health Survey collaborators); 2017; Posttraumatic stress disorder in the World Mental Health Surveys; Psychological Medicine 47(13):2260–2274; DOI 10.1017/S0033291717000708; cross-national cross-sectional population survey analysis (26 WMH surveys, 24 countries); access route OA-PDF (two-column layout text)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: 26 WMH population surveys, 71 083 respondents aged 18+; CIDI assessed trauma exposure and 30-day, 12-month and lifetime PTSD. Cross-national lifetime PTSD prevalence 3.9% (total sample), 5.6% among trauma-exposed; half of cases persistent; treatment seeking 53.5% in high-income vs 22.8% / 28.7% in lower-income groups; social disadvantage associated with lifetime PTSD.
- Introduction: 70% of WMH populations report trauma exposure; prior country estimates of lifetime PTSD vary widely (1.7% South Korea to 9.2% Canada with worst-event method; 2.3–3.4% with random-event method); need for standardised cross-national data.
- Methods: 18 nationally representative and 8 metropolitan surveys, fielded 2001–2012, World Bank income groups; Part I (123 299) and Part II (71 083) interviews, average response rate 70.6% (45.9–97.2%); CIDI 3.0, DSM-IV criteria; 29 trauma types; PTSD assessed for worst event and a randomly selected event, with weighting to represent all lifetime TEs; six prevalence measures; survival models and logistic regression; Taylor series SEs (SUDAAN).
- Results: Lifetime PTSD in population 3.9%; high-income countries 5.0%, upper-middle 2.3%, low-lower middle 2.1%. Table 1 country rows: USA 6.9% lifetime in total sample (8.3% among exposed), Australia 7.3%, New Zealand 6.1%, Northern Ireland 8.8%, Germany 1.7%, Japan 1.3%. Among exposed: lifetime 5.6%, 12-month 2.8%, 30-day 1.4%. Persistence (Table 2): 12-month among lifetime cases 50.5%. Earlier onset in high-income countries (median ~25–28 vs 43). Treatment (Table 3). Correlates (Table 4): female OR 2.6 for lifetime PTSD among exposed; younger age, unmarried, low education/income, not employed.
- Discussion: Wide range 0.5–14.5% among exposed; half persistent; treatment gaps; later AOO than other anxiety/mood disorders; prevention and screening strategies.
- Limitations stated by authors: Variable response rates (post-stratification used); retrospective assessment may under-report lifetime TE and PTSD and misestimate AOO; cultural differences in disclosure; few countries with recent mass violence (PTSD much higher there, e.g. 37.4% in Algeria).
- Supplementary material: not available (online Tables S1–S4 referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Large, standardised, population-based, peer-reviewed; DSM-IV lifetime PTSD by CIDI with consistent methodology. Retrospective lay-interview assessment likely underestimates lifetime prevalence; random-event approach gives lower estimates than worst-event surveys. Country heterogeneity large. The GWAS used (PGC-PTSD Freeze 3) is dominated by European-ancestry and US/military cohorts, for which the high-income or US figures are more relevant than the cross-national pool. Not MR.

## Relevance to this manuscript
M3 trait-by-trait:
- AD (0.05): Not addressed.
- DLB (0.005): Not addressed.
- MDD (0.15): Not addressed (only mentioned in introduction as context).
- PTSD (assumed 0.07): Gives a lower cross-national value, but the US value matches the assumption. Abstract: "The cross-national lifetime prevalence of PTSD was 3.9% in the total sample and 5.6% among the trauma exposed." High-income countries 5.0% (Table 1 row, whitespace collapsed: "High income countries 3107 5 0.1"). United States: Table 1 row "The USA 602 6.9 0.4" = lifetime PTSD 6.9% in the total US sample (NCS-R). Thus K = 0.07 is supported only as a US/high-trauma-exposure figure; a cross-national or high-income K would be 0.039–0.05. Recommend stating that 0.07 approximates US lifetime prevalence (6.9%) and running a sensitivity value (e.g. 0.04–0.05).
- Insomnia (0.10): Not addressed.
- SCZ (0.01): Not addressed.
Also relevant to D6 (lifetime vs late-life phenotype, retrospective reporting).

## Decision
KEEP — primary, standardised cross-national source for lifetime DSM-IV PTSD prevalence; supports K = 0.07 as the US figure and gives lower cross-national/high-income values for sensitivity analysis.
