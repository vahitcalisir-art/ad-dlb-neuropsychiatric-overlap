## Bibliographic record
Saha S, Chant D, Welham J, McGrath J; 2005; A systematic review of the prevalence of schizophrenia; PLoS Medicine 2(5):e141; DOI 10.1371/journal.pmed.0020141; systematic review (MOOSE-conforming, distributional synthesis without pooling); access route OA-PDF (two-column layout text)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: Studies published 1965–2002; 1,721 prevalence estimates from 188 studies in 46 countries (~154,140 potentially overlapping cases); 132 core, 15 migrant, 41 special-group studies. Median per 1,000 (10–90% quantiles): point 4.6 (1.9–10.0), period 3.3 (1.3–8.2), lifetime 4.0 (1.6–12.1), lifetime morbid risk (LMR) 7.2 (3.1–27.1). No sex or urbanicity difference; migrants higher (ratio 1.8); least developed countries lower; higher-quality studies higher.
- Introduction: Incidence median 15.2/100,000; definitions of point, period, lifetime prevalence and LMR (LMR includes those deceased, closer to cumulative incidence); prior review (Goldner 2002) pooled 1-year 3.4 and lifetime 5.5 per 1,000.
- Methods: Electronic search (MEDLINE, PsycINFO, EMBASE, LILACS), reference checking, author contact; inclusion of primary prevalence data; prison and family-genetic studies excluded; sequential filters (core/migrant/special; prevalence type; discrete estimates); quality score; cumulative distribution plots; medians, quantiles; harmonic means compared by mixed models on log scale; planned sensitivity analyses with Bonferroni correction.
- Results: Table 5 lifetime prevalence persons median 4.00 per 1,000 (20 studies, 29 estimates; 10–90% 1.83–11.55); Table 6 LMR persons median 7.20 (mean 11.88); point, period, lifetime not significantly different; LMR significantly higher than lifetime (p = 0.04); combined persons median 3.30; male:female ratio median 1.11; least developed countries median 2.62 vs developed 3.30; high-quality tercile median 5.18; homeless very high, Amish/Hutterites very low.
- Discussion: Medians consistent with Torrey (4.6/1,000); authors state DSM-IV's lifetime prevalence of 0.5%–1% is an overestimate (this passage is split across interleaved columns in the text file, so it is not quoted as a claim); oft-quoted one in a hundred derives from LMR and is also an overestimate — LMR is seven to eight per 1,000; median preferred over arithmetic mean (11.9) for skewed data; no sex difference despite male incidence excess.
- Limitations stated by authors: Possible missed studies/data entry errors; ad hoc filter rules; persons distributions higher than sex-specific for LMR/inpatient data (unexplained); combined analyses not based on discrete data; standard errors available for only 26% of estimates (no formal meta-analysis); heterogeneous mixed urban/rural category.
- Supplementary material: not available (Tables S1–S6, Dataset S1 referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Comprehensive, peer-reviewed systematic review with explicit definitions of prevalence types; no pooled estimate or CI (distributional approach); heterogeneous older studies (1965–2002), many non-European. Medians probably underestimate true lifetime risk relative to register-based cumulative incidence, but this is the standard citation for schizophrenia prevalence. Not MR.

## Relevance to this manuscript
M3 trait-by-trait:
- AD (0.05): Not addressed.
- DLB (0.005): Not addressed.
- MDD (0.15): Not addressed.
- PTSD (0.07): Not addressed.
- Insomnia (0.10): Not addressed.
- SCZ (assumed 0.01): Gives a different (lower) value. Lifetime prevalence median 4.0 per 1,000 (0.004) and LMR median 7.2 per 1,000 (0.0072): "is 4.0 per 1,000; and the lifetime morbid risk (the likelihood that a particular individual will develop schizophrenia in their lifetime) is 7.2 per 1,000." The authors explicitly call the one-in-a-hundred figure an overestimate. Hence K = 0.01 is not supported as a central estimate by this source; it lies within the upper part of the LMR distribution (90% quantile 27.1 per 1,000; arithmetic mean 11.9). If the manuscript keeps K = 0.01 (the convention used by PGC for liability-scale h²), it should say so and cite Saha et al. for the lower LMR (~0.007) or run a sensitivity value; Steiger filtering is insensitive to this small difference.

## Decision
KEEP — standard systematic-review source for schizophrenia prevalence; needed to justify (or qualify) the SCZ K value, while noting it supports ~0.007 (LMR) rather than 0.01.
