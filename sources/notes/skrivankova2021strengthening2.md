## Bibliographic record
Skrivankova VW, Richmond RC, Woolf BAR, et al.; 2021; Strengthening the reporting of observational studies in epidemiology using mendelian randomisation (STROBE-MR): explanation and elaboration; BMJ 375:n2233; DOI 10.1136/bmj.n2233; reporting guideline (explanation and elaboration document); access route PMC-XML (PMC8546498)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: MR evidence often hampered by inadequate reporting; STROBE-MR assists transparent reporting; this E&E explains the 20 checklist items with rationale, glossary and examples.
- Introduction: Confounding and reverse causation in observational epidemiology; MR uses germline variants as instruments; Box 1 scope (designs addressed: one- and two-sample MR, multiple exposures/outcomes; not GWAS, sequencing, expression studies); Table 2 glossary (two-sample MR, bidirectional MR, IV assumptions, strand alignment/palindromic SNPs, LD, instrument strength, horizontal pleiotropy, weak instrument bias with caveat on F<10, collider bias, winner's curse).
- Methods (of guideline): Developed from 2018; Bristol workshop May 2019; preprint July 2019; revised after comments. Table 3 checklist: items 1 title/abstract; 2–3 background/objectives; 4a–e design and data sources (setting, participants, genetic variants QC/selection, outcome definitions, ethics); 5 assumptions; 6a–e statistical methods (variables, variants/weights, estimator, missing data, multiple testing); 7 assessment of assumptions; 8 sensitivity analyses; 9 software and pre-registration; 10a–d descriptive data incl. two-sample similarity and overlap; 11a–d main results; 12a–b assumption assessment and heterogeneity statistics (I², Q, E value); 13a–e sensitivity, direction (bidirectional MR), comparison with non-MR, leave-one-out plots; 14–17 discussion (key results, limitations, interpretation, generalisability); 18–20 funding, data sharing, conflicts.
- Results (examples and explanations): Box 2 describes core IV assumptions plus homogeneity/monotonicity, violations (population stratification, selection/collider bias, dynastic effects, assortative mating), assumptions of MR-Egger (InSIDE), weighted median and mode, and the two-sample assumption of equal SNP–exposure associations. Box 3 and Table 5 give assessments (F statistic, MR-Egger intercept, median/mode, negative controls, heterogeneity as joint test). Item 10d: overlap bias linear in overlapping individuals. Box 4: binary exposures should be interpreted as genetic liability; MR typically estimates lifetime effects.
- Discussion: STROBE-MR is a minimum reporting set, not a quality scale or conduct guideline; living document at strobe-mr.org; EQUATOR inclusion.
- Limitations stated by authors: not a formal quality-assessment tool; some items do not apply to non-IV MR designs; pre-registration rare in secondary data analysis.
- Supplementary material: not available (web appendix 1 checklist, web appendix 2 additional examples referenced)

## Quality appraisal
Peer-reviewed consensus reporting guideline (BMJ) by leading MR methodologists and journal editors; funding declared, no relevant financial conflicts. Not empirical research; sample size not applicable. Consistent with methods papers in this batch.

## Relevance to this manuscript
M2: citation for adherence to STROBE-MR (checklist items: assumptions, software, overlap reporting, bidirectional MR, sensitivity analyses). D6: interpretation of binary psychiatric exposures as genetic liability, lifetime-effect interpretation (lifetime vs late-life phenotype), two-sample population similarity, weak-instrument caveat beyond F=10.

## Decision
KEEP — reporting guideline the study follows (STROBE-MR); the E&E document supports reporting and interpretation choices.
