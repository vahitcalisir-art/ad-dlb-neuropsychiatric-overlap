## Bibliographic record
Sassi KLM, Thomson A, Young VM, et al.; 2026; Mild behavioral impairment and risk of incident dementia: A systematic review and meta-analysis; Journal of Alzheimer's Disease 113(4):1587-1601; DOI 10.1177/13872877261480315; systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of longitudinal cohorts (PRISMA, OSF-preregistered); access route PMC-XML (PMC13688341)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: systematic review of longitudinal studies on MBI and incident dementia; five databases to Nov 2025; MBI associated with increased dementia risk; apathy and affective dysregulation strongest domains; biomarker links to amyloid, plasma p-tau, structural change.
- Introduction: NPS long seen as manifestations of established disease; psychiatric disorders, especially depression, considered dementia risk factors; NPS frequently emerge years before cognitive impairment; ISTAART MBI criteria (2016): later-life onset, persistence >=6 months, distinct from chronic psychiatric disorders.
- Methods: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, PsycInfo, Scielo to 9 Nov 2025; adults >=50 without dementia; ISTAART-aligned MBI; outcome incident dementia; two independent reviewers; Newcastle-Ottawa risk of bias; HR-only random-effects meta-analysis (metafor), I2, funnel plot, Egger test; OR studies excluded.
- Results: 5271 records, 62 full texts, 11 studies in synthesis, 8 in meta-analysis (9 effect sizes). MBI prevalence 3.5% to 39.3% in dementia-free samples. Individual HRs e.g. 2.42 (ADNI, conversion to AD), 3.92 (ADNI, 5-year dementia), apathy HR 5.91 (NC) vs 2.16 (MCI), affective dysregulation HR 2.59 (NC) / 1.50 (MCI). Pooled HR 2.70 (95% CI 2.07-3.51), I2 = 89.8%, Egger z = 3.41, p = 0.0006 (funnel asymmetry). AD most frequent subtype (>80% in NACC studies); few DLB conversions.
- Discussion: MBI as early clinical manifestation (prodrome) of dementia; AD predominance likely reflects base rates; MBI not AD-specific; APOE e4 modifies associations paradoxically; race and sleep disturbance modify risk.
- Limitations stated by authors: heterogeneity of designs and instruments precluding sensitivity/subgroup analyses; informant-based NPI/NPI-Q reporting bias; variable follow-up; subtypes often unreported; limited biomarker integration precludes causal temporal inference; unclear overlap of MBI with depression and other psychiatric disorders; publication bias.
- Supplementary material: not available (search strategy, excluded studies, NOS table referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Meta-analysis of 8 cohorts (mostly retrospective, largely NACC/ADNI with possible overlapping participants), substantial heterogeneity (I2 89.8%) and evidence of publication bias; NOS risk rated low. Peer-reviewed (J Alzheimers Dis). Not MR; observational associations susceptible to reverse causation (which the authors frame as prodromal expression). Consistent with NACC MBI estimates cited in other reviews (e.g. 2.76-fold).

## Relevance to this manuscript
I1: best quantitative source in this batch for MBI as early manifestation of dementia (pooled HR 2.70). I2: supports the reverse-causation/prodromal interpretation of late-life psychiatric symptoms preceding dementia, and notes depression as a putative risk factor and unresolved overlap with psychiatric disorders. D6: lifetime vs late-life phenotype distinction (MBI explicitly distinguished from chronic psychiatric disorders).

## Decision
KEEP — peer-reviewed, preregistered meta-analysis giving the pooled HR for MBI and incident dementia and an explicit prodromal framing (I1, I2, D6).
