## Bibliographic record
Jansen IE, Savage JE, Watanabe K, et al.; 2019 (published version; the text file read is the bioRxiv preprint doi:10.1101/258533, posted 22 Feb 2018, titled "Genetic meta-analysis identifies 9 novel loci and functional pathways for Alzheimer's disease risk"); Genome-wide meta-analysis identifies new loci and functional pathways influencing Alzheimer's disease risk; Nature Genetics 51(3):404-413; DOI 10.1038/s41588-018-0311-9; GWAS meta-analysis (clinical AD case-control + UK Biobank AD-by-proxy) with LDSC genetic correlation and GSMR Mendelian randomization; access route: OA-PDF (bioRxiv preprint text)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: Largest GWAS of clinically diagnosed AD plus AD-by-proxy (71,880 cases, 383,378 controls). AD-by-proxy is based on parental AD diagnosis and shows rg=0.81 with clinical AD. 29 risk loci (9 novel), 215 potential causative genes; expression enriched in immune tissues/cells (spleen, liver, microglia); lipid-related and APP-degradation gene sets; genetic correlations with several health outcomes; MR suggests a protective effect of cognitive ability on AD.
- Introduction: AD heritability 60-80% (twin studies); APOE strongest locus; IGAP (Lambert 2013) found 19 further loci. Study design: 455,258 European-ancestry individuals in 3 phases: phase 1 clinical AD (24,087 cases, 55,058 controls); phase 2 UKB AD-by-proxy based on parental AD status; phase 3 meta-analysis plus deCODE replication. Proxy approach motivated by Liu et al. 2017. Proxy cases = one or two parents with AD (N=47,793; the text also gives 74,793 once, an internal inconsistency), more weight for 2 affected parents; proxy controls = 2 unaffected parents (N=328,320), older cognitively normal parents weighted more. Authors state the proxy is not a pure measure of the individual's AD status and effect sizes will be somewhat underestimated, but the large N increases power.
- Methods: (design) multivariate meta-analysis correcting for sample overlap with the cross-trait LDSC intercept (mvGWAMA), weighting by sqrt(N). (sample) Clinical cohorts: PGC-ALZ (Norwegian DemGene, Swedish STSA, TwinGene; 2,736 cases/14,471 controls after QC), IGAP stage 1 summary statistics (17,008 cases/37,154 controls), ADSP whole-exome sequencing (4,343 cases/3,163 controls after duplicate removal vs IGAP); diagnoses by NINCDS-ADRDA, NIA-AA, ICD-10 or registers. UKB second release (July 2017), unrelated European-ancestry participants (ancestry by projection onto 1000 Genomes PCs, Mahalanobis distance), final N=364,859 for the proxy GWAS (M age 56.5, 54.0% female). (phenotype) Proxy = linear count of affected biological parents (0-2) from self-reported parental Alzheimer's disease/dementia; each unaffected parent contributes weight (100-age)/100, capped at 0.32 (max population prevalence of AD); 32,327 mothers (8.6%) and 17,014 fathers (4.5%) affected; 47,793 participants (12.7%) with one or both parents affected; 393 participants with own ICD-10 G30/F00 hospital/death record coded as maximum score 2. Analysed by linear regression (PLINK) with 12 PCs, age, sex, array, centre. Clinical cohorts imputed to 1000 Genomes Phase 3; UKB imputed to HRC/1000G+UK10K. Positional mapping uses GRCh37/hg19. (statistics) LDSC h2 and rg (HapMap3 SNPs, 1000G EUR LD scores); rg with 41 traits (Bonferroni 0.05/41); GSMR with HEIDI outlier filtering, one direction only (exposures -> AD), because AD is late-onset. Replication in deCODE (6,593 cases, 174,289 controls) and PGS in ADDNeuroMed (761 individuals).
- Results: Phase 1 clinical AD: h2SNP (liability, LDSC) 0.055 (SE 0.0099); LDSC intercept 1.044; 18 loci. Phase 2 proxy: intercept 1.022, lambda 1.071; 13 loci, 8 overlapping with clinical loci. rg clinical vs proxy = 0.81 (SE 0.185); sign concordance 96.1% of clinical lead SNPs in UKB. Phase 3: N=455,258 (71,880 (proxy) cases, 383,378 (proxy) controls), intercept 1.0018; 29 loci, 9 novel; 4 Lambert loci (MEF2C, NME8, CELF1, FERMT2) not replicated at GWS, mostly due to weaker UKB signal. deCODE: 6/7 testable novel loci same direction, 4 nominally significant. PGS explains 7.1% of variance in clinical AD (3.9% excluding APOE). Gene sets: protein lipid complex, regulation of APP catabolic process, HDL particle; tissues: whole blood, spleen, lung; microglia. Cross-trait rg (Figure 4D, Supplementary Table 19): adult cognitive ability rg=-0.22, age at first birth rg=-0.33, educational attainment rg=-0.25, previous AD GWAS rg=0.90; no significant psychiatric-trait rg reported in the main text. GSMR: protective effects of cognitive ability (OR 0.89, 95% CI 0.85-0.92), educational attainment (OR 0.88, 0.81-0.94) and height (OR 0.96, 0.94-0.97) on AD.
- Discussion: The non-conventional by-proxy strategy is described as robust given rg=0.81 and deCODE replication; emphasises causal role of immune system (microglia) and lipid-inflammation interplay; cognitive reserve/education interpretation of MR results.
- Limitations stated by authors: proxy phenotype is not a pure measure of individual AD status, may include individuals who never develop AD, so effect sizes are underestimated; unaffected (especially younger) parents may still develop AD (addressed by age weighting); UKB own-diagnosis records are few (393) and of limited representativeness; some established loci lost GWS because of lower signal in UKB; reverse-direction MR not tested because AD is late-onset; LDSC h2 is conservative.
- Supplementary material: not available (supplementary tables 1-20 not in the text file; only supplementary figure captions present)

## Quality appraisal
Very large sample (455,258; 71,880 cases incl. proxy), adequate for GWAS and LDSC. Bias risks: proxy phenotype misclassification (parental self-report of "Alzheimer's disease/dementia", which mixes dementia types), age weighting assumptions, survival/selection of UKB volunteers; clinical cohorts partly overlap (IGAP/ADSP), handled by duplicate removal and LDSC-intercept correction. rg between proxy and clinical AD is high but below 1 with a large SE (0.185). MR (GSMR) one-directional with HEIDI filtering; no psychiatric exposures tested. Published version is peer-reviewed (Nature Genetics 2019), but the text file read is the non-peer-reviewed preprint; numbers (e.g., proxy case count 47,793 vs 74,793 printed in the introduction) should be checked against the published article before citing. Superseded as primary AD GWAS by Bellenguez 2022, which incorporates UKB proxy cases.

## Relevance to this manuscript
D2: core source describing how AD-by-proxy cases are constructed from parental history (weighting by parental age, cap 0.32), the number of proxy cases/controls, the imperfect proxy-clinical genetic correlation (rg=0.81), and the authors' own statement that proxy phenotypes dilute effect sizes and include individuals who never develop AD; useful to discuss bias in Bellenguez 2022 (which contains UKB proxy cases) and to justify the FinnGen clinical-AD sensitivity analysis. M1: background on the UKB proxy component and European ancestry of AD GWAS. I3/D1: LDSC rg of AD with 41 traits (significant only for cognition, education, age at first birth; no psychiatric trait reported significant) and one-directional MR (exposure -> AD) as prior genetic evidence. D6: European-only ancestry, late-onset phenotype rationale for not testing reverse MR.

## Decision
KEEP — peer-reviewed landmark AD-by-proxy GWAS that directly supports the D2 discussion of proxy versus clinical AD phenotypes (note: verify figures against the published Nature Genetics version, since the text read is the preprint).
