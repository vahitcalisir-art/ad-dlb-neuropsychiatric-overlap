## Bibliographic record
Meng M, Shen X, Xie Y, et al.; 2025; Insomnia and risk of all-cause dementia: A systematic review and meta-analysis; PLOS One 20(4):e0318814; DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0318814; systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort and nested case-control studies (PRISMA, PROSPERO CRD42024502980); access route PMC-XML (PMC11981150)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: 16 studies, >9 million individuals; insomnia associated with all-cause dementia OR 1.36 (1.01-1.84), AD OR 1.52 (1.19-1.93), VD OR 2.10 (2.06-2.14); insomnia subtypes not associated; risk higher with <5 y follow-up (OR 2.16) than >=5 y (OR 1.17); heterogeneity persisted.
- Introduction: insomnia most common sleep disorder; DSM-5 definition; prior meta-analyses conflicting; mechanisms (inflammation, amyloid clearance, tau); complex bidirectional relationship between insomnia and dementia.
- Methods: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane to 15 Jan 2024; cohort or cohort-based case-control studies with adjusted HR/RR/OR; NOS quality; random effects if I2 > 50%; leave-one-out; subgroup and meta-regression; Egger and Begg tests; Stata 14 and R.
- Results: 16 studies (11 cohort, 5 case-control; 1994-2023; total 9,016,761; 11 retrospective). Insomnia mostly self-reported (9 studies) or ICD codes (6). All-cause dementia OR 1.36 (1.01-1.84), I2 = 98.7% (8 studies); AD OR 1.52 (1.19-1.93), I2 = 65.8% (3 studies); VD OR 2.10 (2 studies). Initial insomnia OR 1.01 (results text gives 95% CI 0.77-1.31, abstract gives 0.71-1.31), sleep-maintenance 0.88, early awakening 0.94. Subgroups (Table 3): follow-up >=5 y 1.17 (1.03-1.33) vs <5 y 2.16 (1.81-2.60); Asia 2.19, Europe 1.24, Americas 1.05; ICD 1.89 vs self-report 1.11; prospective 1.17. Meta-regression found no significant moderator. No publication bias (Egger p = 0.204).
- Discussion: mechanisms (glymphatic amyloid clearance, tau, inflammation/vascular); regional differences possibly from APOE e4 frequency, culture, health care; authors prefer the >=5-year follow-up estimate as more realistic given long preclinical AD phase.
- Limitations stated by authors: unexplained clinical and methodological heterogeneity; reliance on self-reported insomnia (recall bias, misclassification); unmeasured confounding; results to be interpreted with caution.
- Supplementary material: not available (search strategies, NOS details, sensitivity plots referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Large pooled sample but dominated by one Korean registry (8.4 million); very high heterogeneity (I2 98.7%); AD estimate from only 3 studies; mixes HR/RR/OR as OR; inconsistent CI between abstract and results for initial insomnia; minor reporting errors (forest plot labels). Peer-reviewed (PLOS One, two review rounds). Not MR. The attenuation with longer follow-up is consistent with reverse causation/prodromal insomnia but the authors do not frame it that way explicitly.

## Relevance to this manuscript
I2: provides the pooled observational estimate linking insomnia to all-cause dementia and AD, and evidence that associations are stronger with shorter follow-up (compatible with reverse causation), and notes the bidirectional relationship. Complements livingston2020dementia (sleep disturbance RR 1.2/1.6) with an insomnia-specific estimate. D6: self-reported vs ICD-defined insomnia differences relevant to phenotype definition (our insomnia GWAS is questionnaire-based in UK Biobank).

## Decision
KEEP — the most specific insomnia-dementia/AD meta-analysis in this batch, with numeric estimates and a follow-up-length gradient supporting the reverse-causation argument (I2, D6).
