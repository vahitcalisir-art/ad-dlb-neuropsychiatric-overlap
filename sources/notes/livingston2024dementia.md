## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract (no structured abstract in the accepted manuscript; Strapline and Key messages serve as summary): 14 modifiable risk factors, nearly half of dementia risk potentially preventable; recommendations for prevention across the life course and for care after diagnosis.
- Introduction (Strapline, Key messages, Prevention, Lifecourse nature of exposures, Length and timing of exposure): 14 potentially modifiable risk factors (vision impairment and high LDL cholesterol added to the 12 of 2020) account for nearly half of dementia risk; long preclinical phase of AD; risks identified in the few years before dementia may reflect causation or reverse causation; recommends reporting follow-up separately for incident cases or excluding cases within 5–10 years.
- Methods (Total PAF calculation; commission meta-analyses): new random-effects meta-analyses for selected factors (e.g. depression, seven studies with 10–14-year follow-up), PAF with communality weighting; evidence graded narratively by the commissioners.
- Results (Specific potentially modifiable risk factors; other risk factors under consideration): depression RR 2.25 (1.69–2.98; I² 82.8%) at 10–14-year follow-up, risk at all adult ages, partly preclinical in late life, classified as midlife risk factor; sleep: short sleep weakly associated, long sleep largely reverse causation, insomnia not associated in one Norwegian cohort; bipolar disorder consistently associated; psychotic disorders including schizophrenia RR 2.52 (I² 99.7%), unclear whether independent of other risk factors; anxiety; PTSD: one meta-analysis HR 1.61 (1.43–1.81; I² 85.8%), too heterogeneous to classify as modifiable risk factor; menopause/HRT; multimorbidity and frailty.
- Discussion (Interventions and care in dementia; Diagnosis; Biomarkers; Anti-amyloid antibodies; neuropsychiatric symptoms in dementia; Technology; Conclusions): diagnosis pathways, biomarkers, anti-amyloid antibody trials and limitations, multicomponent care, treatment of depression, psychosis and agitation in dementia; conclusions restate the prevention potential.
- Limitations stated by authors: much evidence observational with residual confounding and reverse causation; heterogeneity of meta-analyses; PAF assumptions.
- Supplementary material: not consulted (claims used are in the main text).

## Quality appraisal
Authoritative, widely cited consensus report by an international commission; the risk-factor sections summarise observational evidence and new meta-analyses with high heterogeneity, which the report states. Suitable for background statements on the observational associations of the four psychiatric exposures with dementia and on reverse causation; not used for effect sizes in any calculation. Accepted manuscript version, so wording could differ slightly from the typeset article.
