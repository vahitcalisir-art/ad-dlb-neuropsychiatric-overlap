## Bibliographic record
GBD 2019 Mental Disorders Collaborators (Ferrari AJ, Santomauro DF, Mantilla Herrera AM, et al.); 2022; Global, regional, and national burden of 12 mental disorders in 204 countries and territories, 1990–2019: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2019; The Lancet Psychiatry 9(2):137–150; DOI 10.1016/S2215-0366(21)00395-3; systematic analysis / modelling study (GBD 2019); access route PMC-XML (PMC8776563)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: Mental disorders DALYs rose from 80·8 million (1990) to 125·3 million (2019), 4·9% of global DALYs; age-standardised rates stable; mental disorders among top ten causes of burden.
- Introduction: Lancet Commission on global mental health; last comprehensive review based on GBD 2010.
- Methods: Disorders: depressive disorders (MDD plus dysthymia), anxiety disorders (all subtypes combined), bipolar, schizophrenia, autism, conduct disorder, ADHD, eating disorders, idiopathic intellectual disability, other (personality); case definitions mostly DSM-IV-TR / ICD-10; included probability-sample general-population surveys from 1980; prevalence inputs were past-year prevalence or shorter, lifetime prevalence accepted only for bipolar and autism (recall bias); bias adjustment by MR-BRT network meta-analysis; DisMod-MR 2.1 with location covariates (conflict mortality, Gallup Negative Experience Index, IPV/CSA attributable MDD burden) for MDD and anxiety; severity proportions, disability weights, comorbidity simulation; YLLs only for eating disorders.
- Results: 970·1 million cases in 2019. Table 1 (2019, age-standardised per 100 000): depressive disorders 3440·1, anxiety 3779·5, bipolar 489·8, schizophrenia 287·4. Depressive disorders 37·3% of mental-disorder DALYs, anxiety 22·9%, schizophrenia 12·2%. Regional Table 2 (note: in the PMC text the column headings are shifted; the first numeric column is all mental disorders, the second schizophrenia, the third depressive disorders). Depressive disorders ranked 13th DALY cause.
- Discussion: Burden concentrated in working ages; high DALYs where childhood sexual abuse, IPV, conflict are common; COVID-19 likely to increase burden; treatment gaps.
- Limitations stated by authors: Sparse data and scarce high-quality surveys; unquantified measurement error and cross-cultural comparability; severity distributions from few high-income studies; DSM-IV/ICD-10 basis; personality, binge eating not fully captured; no substance use/neurological disorders; mortality gap not captured in YLLs; independent comorbidity assumption.
- Supplementary material: not available (appendix referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Large, peer-reviewed, systematic data base; but estimates are model-based point/past-year prevalences, which are conceptually different from the lifetime prevalence used for liability-scale h² conversion of case-control GWAS. PTSD is not estimated separately (inside combined anxiety disorders); insomnia not included. Possible text-extraction column misalignment in Table 2. Not MR.

## Relevance to this manuscript
M3 trait-by-trait:
- AD (0.05): Not addressed (neurological disorders excluded).
- DLB (0.005): Not addressed.
- MDD (0.15): Gives a different, non-lifetime value. Table 1 row (copied from text): "Depressive disorders Total 170·8 (152·7–190·4) 3486·2 (3140·8–3855·7) 279·6 (251·6–310·3) 3440·1 (3097·0–3817·6)", i.e. 2019 age-standardised point/past-year prevalence 3440·1 per 100 000 (~3.4%, all ages, MDD plus dysthymia). Does not support a lifetime value of 0.15; the authors explicitly did not model lifetime prevalence for depression.
- PTSD (0.07): Not addressed (only "anxiety disorders (a combined estimate of all subtypes)").
- Insomnia (0.10): Not addressed.
- SCZ (0.01): Gives a different, non-lifetime value. Table 1 row: "Schizophrenia Total 14·2 (12·2–16·5) 289·9 (249·8–333·2) 23·6 (20·2–27·2) 287·4 (246·2–330·9)", i.e. 287·4 per 100 000 (~0.29%) age-standardised in 2019. Does not support 0.01 as lifetime prevalence.
Could be cited only to explain why GBD figures are not used for K (point vs lifetime prevalence).

## Decision
DROP — reports point/past-year prevalence only (lifetime explicitly excluded for MDD and SCZ), no PTSD or insomnia estimate; lifetime-based sources (bromet2011cross, koenen2017posttraumatic, saha2005systematic) are more appropriate for K values.
