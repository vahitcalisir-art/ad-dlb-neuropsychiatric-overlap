## Bibliographic record
Bromet E, Andrade LH, Hwang I, et al.; 2011; Cross-national epidemiology of DSM-IV major depressive episode; BMC Medicine 9:90; DOI 10.1186/1741-7015-9-90; cross-national cross-sectional population survey analysis (WHO World Mental Health Survey Initiative, 18 countries); access route OA-PDF (BMC open access, two-column layout text)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: 18 countries, n = 89,037 adults, CIDI-assessed DSM-IV MDE; average lifetime / 12-month prevalence 14.6% / 5.5% (ten high-income) and 11.1% / 5.9% (eight low- to middle-income); mean AOO 25.7 vs 24.0; female:male ratio about 2:1; separation (high-income) and divorce/widowhood (LMIC) strongest correlates.
- Introduction: Prior cross-national studies (DIS, ICPE CIDI) show wide variation (lifetime 1.0–19.0%); question whether cross-national differences reflect CIDI threshold differences; examines screening stem endorsement and conditional prevalence.
- Methods: Face-to-face lay interviews, WMH CIDI 3.0; ten high-income (incl. USA NCS-R, ESEMeD countries, Japan, Israel, New Zealand) and eight LMIC surveys; Part I/II design with weights; DSM-IV MDE defined without organic exclusions and without diagnostic hierarchy rules; WHO-DAS-II impairment; logistic regression for correlates of 12-month MDE; Taylor series SEs. Sample sizes 2,372–12,790; weighted response rate 71.7%; most samples aged 18+ (Colombia, Mexico 18–65).
- Results: Table 2 lifetime prevalence: high-income total 14.6%; USA 19.2%, France 21.0%, Netherlands 17.9%, New Zealand 17.8%, Germany 9.9%, Italy 9.9%, Japan 6.6%; LMIC total 11.1% (China 6.5% to Brazil 18.4%). 12-month 5.5% vs 5.9%; 12-month/lifetime ratio 37.7% vs 53.3%; 30-day 1.8% vs 2.6%. Impairment increased with recency (Table 3). Correlates in Tables 4–5 (women OR 1.8 high-income, 2.1 LMIC).
- Discussion: Variation not explained by stem-question endorsement; positive prevalence-impairment correlation; lifetime-vs-12-month pattern may reflect recall error in LMIC.
- Limitations stated by authors: Cross-sectional (no temporal ordering of correlates); variable response rates; some metropolitan-only samples; excluded institutionalised, military, cognitively impaired people; survivor bias; rates are conservative estimates; Nigeria excluded because of extremely low prevalence; CIDI structure may underestimate depression in some settings; clinical reappraisal not done in all countries; retrospective recall error for lifetime episodes.
- Supplementary material: not available (none referenced beyond tables)

## Quality appraisal
Large, standardised, population-based, peer-reviewed. Phenotype is DSM-IV major depressive episode (no hierarchy exclusions, so some bipolar episodes included), not strictly MDD; lay CIDI interviews; lifetime estimates retrospective (likely conservative). For a European-ancestry GWAS (PGC MDD), the high-income pooled value is the most relevant. Not MR.

## Decision
KEEP — standardised cross-national lifetime DSM-IV MDE prevalence; the high-income value (14.6%) directly supports K = 0.15 for MDD.
