## Bibliographic record
Adams MJ, Streit F, Meng X, et al. (Major Depressive Disorder Working Group of the Psychiatric Genomics Consortium); 2025; Trans-ancestry genome-wide study of depression identifies 697 associations implicating cell types and pharmacotherapies; Cell 188(3):640-652.e9; DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2024.12.002; trans-ancestry GWAS meta-analysis (PGC MDD wave 3, "MDD2025"); access route PMC-XML (PMC11829167, NIHMS author manuscript, CC BY)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: GWAS meta-analysis of 688,808 MD cases and 4,364,225 controls from 29 countries, diverse and admixed ancestries; 697 associations at 635 loci (293 novel); 308 high-confidence genes; enrichment of postsynaptic density/receptor clustering; excitatory, inhibitory, medium spiny and amygdala neurons; antidepressant-target enrichment; PGS explain up to 5.8% of liability variance in Europeans.
- Introduction: MD affects ~15% of the global population in their lifetime; twin heritability ~37%; previous largest study (MVP, 23andMe, UK Biobank, FinnGen, iPSYCH; 371K cases) found 243 loci.
- Methods: 109 cohort datasets. Core PGC MDD49 mega-analysis (28,147 cases / 48,033 controls, European, individual-level data) plus 24 European summary-statistic cohorts (496,710 cases / 3,010,973 controls) plus diverse-ancestry cohorts (AFR 8 cohorts, EAS 7, SAS 1, Hispanic/Latin American 5, 12 mixed-ancestry cohorts analysed jointly with GENESIS). European fixed-effects meta-analysis: 525,197 cases / 3,362,335 controls (76 studies, Neff/2 788,603) used for heritability, gene prioritisation, enrichment and genetic correlation. Phenotyping: clinical/interview, EHR, questionnaire, self-reported diagnosis; some cases do not meet formal MDD criteria, so the term "major depression (MD)" is used. Share of effective N: 4% clinical/interview, 54% EHR, 14% questionnaire, 27% self-report. Genomic SEM common-factor model across phenotype definitions. Summary statistics aligned to GRCh37/hg19, HRC v1.1 marker names; QC with DENTIST, FST outliers, LDSC cross-cohort intercepts (>0.025 flagged for overlap). COJO with UK Biobank LD reference. SNP-h2 by SBayesS assuming lifetime risk 15%; LDSC rg with OpenGWAS traits (FDR q<0.05). PGS with P+CT and SBayesR/SBayesRC; liability r2 using lifetime prevalence 0.15. Fine-mapping (PolyFun/SuSiE), TWAS/SMR/coloc/FOCUS, PWAS, H-MAGMA, PsyOPS, SynGO, Drug Targetor. Cohort composition named in text/acknowledgments includes 23andMe, MVP, UK Biobank (application 4844), iPSYCH, FinnGen-era cohorts, China Kadoorie Biobank, Estonian Biobank, Genes & Health, HUNT, BioBank Japan.
- Results: 697 independent SNPs in 635 regions (p ≤ 5e-8); European-only 622 SNPs in 570 regions. Phenotype definitions rg 0.78-0.88; common-factor loadings EHR 0.92, questionnaire 0.95, self-report 0.85; only NEGR1 locus heterogeneous (569/570 homogeneous). SNP-h2 8.4% (SE 0.07%) on liability scale (lifetime risk 15%); polygenicity 6%, selection -0.54. PGS r2 liability 5.8% (SE 0.2%), AUC 0.625; OR top vs bottom decile 4.92; public dataset without 23andMe r2 3.9% in Europeans; poor transfer to African ancestry. Genetic correlation results are only deposited on Figshare (not reported in main text); no AD/dementia rg reported in text. Table 1 ancestry breakdown; Table 2 drug enrichments.
- Discussion: largest and most inclusive MD GWAS; DRD2, CYP7B1 etc.; amygdala/hippocampal excitatory neurons; diverse ancestries added 100 novel loci; PGS now capture ~69% of SNP-h2.
- Limitations stated by authors: low proportion of non-European participants (76.6% of MD cases European), arrays designed for European populations; unclear whether architecture differs by ancestry; multi-ancestry fine-mapping exploratory because LD references European. (No explicit discussion of phenotype heterogeneity as a limitation beyond the genomic SEM analysis.)
- Supplementary material: not available (Tables S1-S8, Methods S1 not in the text file; Figure S1 caption only).

## Quality appraisal
Very large sample (688,808 cases; European 525,197 cases), high power. Main bias risks: minimal/self-report phenotyping dominates (only 4% of effective N from clinical/interview), though genomic SEM shows high rg among definitions; inclusion of 23andMe and UK Biobank in the full meta-analysis creates potential sample overlap with other UK Biobank-based GWAS (relevant for MR; our study uses the public file without 23andMe and UK Biobank, but this paper's text only explicitly documents the public file excluding 23andMe - the UK Biobank exclusion of the public file is not stated in the text and must be sourced from the PGC download page/readme). Peer-reviewed (Cell). The public summary-statistic N differs from the headline N; the exact N of the file used must be taken from the file/readme, not from this paper. No direct conflicts with other sources; h2 similar to prior PGC estimates.

## Relevance to this manuscript
- M1: primary description of the MDD GWAS data source (total N, European subset, ancestry breakdown, phenotype definitions and proportions, GRCh37 build, availability of summary statistics without 23andMe, cohorts incl. UK Biobank).
- M3: uses lifetime MD prevalence/risk of 15% (0.15) for liability-scale h2 and PGS r2; introduction cites ~15% lifetime prevalence - supports our MDD prevalence assumption of 0.15.
- D6: authors' limitation on ancestry; self-report/minimal phenotyping and lifetime phenotype; UK Biobank/23andMe content relevant to sample overlap.
- I3: provides SNP-h2 and polygenic architecture; genetic correlations with other traits are only on Figshare (no AD rg in text), so limited direct support for I3.

## Decision
KEEP — this is the MDD GWAS (PGC MDD2025) whose summary statistics the study actually uses; it is the primary data-source citation.
