## Bibliographic record
Zhu CW, Schneider LS, Elder GA, et al.; 2026; Neuropsychiatric symptom profile in neurocognitive disorders and their relationship with functional decline; Frontiers in Neurology 17:1805908; DOI 10.3389/fneur.2026.1805908; longitudinal observational cohort (NACC UDS), Original Research; access route PMC-XML (PMC13381444)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: NACC UDS longitudinal study of clinician-judged NPS and functional decline (FAQ) in AD (N = 11,044), LBD (N = 921) and bvFTD (N = 933). Apathy most common and persistent; depression in >=40% of all groups but not associated with functional decline; psychosis linked to faster decline in AD and LBD.
- Introduction: NPS common across neurocognitive disorders, associated with worse outcomes; most patients develop some NPS but prevalence/persistence vary; psychosis more likely in LBD; disinhibition most common in bvFTD (42.3%) vs AD (30.7%) and LBD (26.3%).
- Methods: NACC UDS Sept 2005 - Sept 2025 freeze; age >=50, MCI or dementia, >=1 follow-up; LBD by McKeith criteria per NACC Guidebook; NPS from clinician B9 form (apathy, depression, hallucinations, delusions, disinhibition, irritability, agitation; psychosis = hallucinations or delusions); FAQ 0-30 outcome; linear mixed models with random intercepts for subject and ADC; covariates incl. APOE e4, CDR, demographics, comorbidities; STROBE.
- Results: AD mean age 73.5, 56.8% APOE e4 carriers; LBD 79.3% male, 40% e4 carriers. NPS frequency: AD apathy 60%, depressed mood 51%; bvFTD apathy 86%, disinhibition 77.6%, agitation 54.2%; LBD apathy 75.4%, psychosis 66.2%, depressed mood 65.2%. Table 2: faster FAQ decline with apathy (0.761, p<=0.001), psychosis (0.527) and agitation (0.232) in AD; apathy in bvFTD (0.809, p=0.005); psychosis in LBD (0.810 +/- 0.303, p=0.007). Depression not associated with rate of decline in any group.
- Discussion: apathy distinct from depression; psychosis in LBD most persistent and linked to accelerated decline, possibly distinct neurobiology; implications for trial design by etiology.
- Limitations stated by authors: NACC not representative (less diverse, more educated, fewer comorbidities); informant-based FAQ; binary NPS indicator ignores temporal order; results not causal; no multiple-comparison correction.
- Supplementary material: not available (only referenced; sensitivity subsamples with >=3/4 visits reported as similar)

## Quality appraisal
Large clinical research cohort (AD 11,044; LBD 921; bvFTD 933), clinician-based diagnoses (not neuropathologically confirmed), clinician-rated binary NPS. Selection bias (research volunteers, mainly White). Not MR. Peer-reviewed (Frontiers in Neurology). Consistent with other literature on high NPS burden in LBD.

## Relevance to this manuscript
I1: quantifies NPS burden (apathy, depression, psychosis) in AD and LBD and links NPS to functional decline. D4: high psychosis (66.2%) and depressed mood (65.2%) in LBD supports the clinical psychiatric profile of DLB. Does not address causation (authors explicitly say not causal), useful for I2 context only.

## Decision
KEEP — primary large-cohort source giving NPS prevalence in both AD and LBD and prognostic link to functional decline (I1, D4).
