## Bibliographic record
Verbanck M, Chen C-Y, Neale B, Do R; 2018; Detection of widespread horizontal pleiotropy in causal relationships inferred from Mendelian randomization between complex traits and diseases; Nature Genetics 50(5):693–698 (Publisher Correction, Nat Genet 2018); DOI 10.1038/s41588-018-0099-7 (PMID 29686387; PMC6083837; NIH author manuscript); methodological paper (new test, simulations, application to 4,250 MR tests across 82 traits); access route PMC-XML (published version, author manuscript). (Preprint title was "Widespread pleiotropy confounds causal relationships between complex traits and diseases inferred from Mendelian randomization".)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: Horizontal pleiotropy (variant affects disease outside its effect on the exposure) can severely bias MR. MR-PRESSO identifies horizontal pleiotropic outliers in multi-instrument summary-level MR; simulations show it is best suited when horizontal pleiotropy occurs in < 50% of instruments. Across complex traits, horizontal pleiotropy was detectable in over 48% of significant causal relationships, distorted causal estimates on average from −131% to 201%, induced false positives in up to 10%, and can be corrected in some but not all instances. (Preprint: 41% and −168% to 189%; "pleiotropy" now "horizontal pleiotropy".)
- Introduction: MR advantages over observational epidemiology (reverse causation, confounding); multi-instrument MR regresses outcome effects on exposure effects; 'no horizontal pleiotropy' (exclusion restriction) assumption; pervasive pleiotropy and genetic correlation among GWAS loci; no systematic evaluation previously; application to 4,250 MR tests from 82 GWAS datasets.
- Methods (Online Methods): IVW fixed-effect meta-analysis = weighted regression with intercept fixed at 0. Three IV assumptions; exclusion-restriction violation = horizontal pleiotropy. Comparators: Q, Q', modified Q/Q' tests; Cook's distance, Studentized residuals; MR-Egger intercept (I²GX informs its use); multivariable MR; weighted median; mode-based estimate. MR-PRESSO global test: leave-one-out IVW slope, observed RSS vs simulated expected RSS, empirical P (K ≥ 1,000 recommended). Outlier test: per-variant observed vs expected RSS, Bonferroni over J variants; requires ≥ 50% valid instruments and InSIDE. Distortion test: D = 100 × (β_original − β_corrected)/|β_corrected|, empirical P from resampling. Simulations: 10,000 per scenario, 50 variants, causal effect 0–0.5, 0–90% pleiotropic variants, positive/balanced, InSIDE satisfied/violated, perfectly overlapping samples. 82 GWAS summary datasets lifted to hg19, Z-scores imputed with ImpG (1000 Genomes Phase 3 EUR, n = 503); 53 traits with instruments → 4,250 tests; 1,000,000 simulations for global/outlier P (Bonferroni 1.17 × 10−5); 10,000 for distortion. R package github.com/rondolab/MR-PRESSO.
- Results: Original Q and Q' tests inflated (5–25%) under null; MR-PRESSO global, Q (modified), Q' (modified) controlled (~5%) and similarly powered; acceptable power when ≥ 10% of variants pleiotropic (Table 1); reduced power with perfectly overlapping samples. Cook's distance FWER > 97% with no pleiotropy; MR-Egger lower precision; I²GX < 90% on average in most settings; weighted median less bias but less precision; all methods biased when InSIDE invalid; MR-PRESSO outlier correction less biased than IVW/MR-Egger/MMR when ≤ 10% pleiotropic, opposite when ≥ 50%. Application: global test significant in 21.69% (n = 922) of 4,250 tests and 48.69% (93/191) of IVW-significant pairs (Table 2); outlier removal corrected 46% (422/922); combined with MMR 47% (438/922) (Table 3); significant distortion in ~10% (22/229) at nominal P (−131% to 201%); false positives 10% (24/229) nominal, 1.2% Bonferroni. Example: BMI→CRP driven by outlier rs2075650 in APOE locus (associated with Alzheimer's disease, lipids, anthropometrics), 12% distortion. (Preprint reported 41% (78/191), −168% to 189%, 100-variant simulations, MR-Egger intercept power < 13%, and no appreciable effect of sample overlap; these preprint statements are not in the published text.)
- Discussion: Horizontal pleiotropy in over 48% of inferred causal relationships; consistent with Hemani et al.; implications for drug discovery; strengths: outlier methods work within IVW, global test powered for small subsets of pleiotropic loci, usable within IVW, MMR and MR-Egger.
- Limitations stated by authors: correction strategies could not always remove pleiotropy (InSIDE violation, unsuitable percentage of pleiotropic variants (> 50% for outlier methods), no applicable covariate, other heterogeneity such as G×G, G×E); overlapping samples across consortia reduce power of the global test; slower processing due to simulations; covariate adjustment requires a priori knowledge.
- Supplementary material: not available

## Quality appraisal
Peer-reviewed (Nature Genetics 2018); text is the NIH author manuscript. Sound simulation design; key assumptions explicit (≥ 50% valid instruments, InSIDE, balanced pleiotropy for outlier test). Authors disclose industry relationships (Deep Genomics, Illumina, Avanir, AstraZeneca, Goldfinch Bio). Consistent with Bowden et al. on MR-Egger limitations (lower precision; I²GX threshold).
Correction checked 2026-11-21 (Publisher Correction 10.1038/s41588-018-0164-2): Supplementary Tables 4, 6, 8 and 10–14 had been missing from the supplementary file; no effect on any claim used here.
