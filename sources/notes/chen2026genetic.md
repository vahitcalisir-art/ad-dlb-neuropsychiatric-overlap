## Bibliographic record
Chen Y, Chen X; 2026; Genetic evidence for a causal role of depression in late-onset Alzheimer disease: A Mendelian randomization study; Medicine (Baltimore) 105(32):e50116; DOI 10.1097/MD.0000000000050116; two-sample MR (summary statistics); access route PMC-XML (PMC13456805)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: Two-sample MR of 7 psychiatric/neurodevelopmental disorders on AD and subtypes (FinnGen). IVW primary; MR Steiger, MR-Egger, MR-PRESSO; supplementary analysis excluding confounder-associated SNPs. Depression -> late-onset AD OR = 1.0736 (95% CI 1.0084–1.1431, P = .0264); after excluding pleiotropic SNPs OR = 1.0726 (1.0069–1.1425, P = .03). All other disorders null (P > .05).
- Introduction: Observational depression–dementia link (~twofold) confounded by reverse causation (late-life depression as prodrome). Prior MR on depression–AD mixed (one positive, MDD-focused studies null). Selection of disorders by prevalence, thematic relevance, and availability within FinnGen.
- Methods: Outcomes FinnGen R12 (clinician ICD codes from registers, explicitly not family-history proxy): broad AD (G6_ALZHEIMER, G30), early-onset AD (AD_EO_EXMORE, 1820 cases), late-onset AD (AD_LO_EXMORE, 9690 cases), dementia in AD (F5_ALZHDEMENT); broad AD 13,964 cases (Discussion). Exposures from IEU OpenGWAS / GWAS Catalog: Depression ebi-a-GCST90018833 (meta-analysis of FinnGen R3 F33 + UK Biobank Phecode 296.22); MDD ebi-a-GCST90086058 (GERA, ICD-9-CM 296.2x/296.3x); bipolar disorder (bip2021, PGC), anxiety disorders, autism, PTSD, UK Biobank family-history items (severe depression in father/siblings). Exact N per exposure only in Table S1 (cases ~1700 to 450,000). European ancestry. IV selection P < 5 × 10^-6 (except bipolar 5 × 10^-8), MAF > 0.01, clumping r2 < 0.001 / 10,000 kb, proxies R2 > 0.8, F > 10. Methods: IVW (OR), MR-Egger, weighted median, weighted mode; Cochran Q; Egger intercept; MR-PRESSO; Steiger filtering; leave-one-out; exclusion of SNPs linked to confounders via GWAS Catalog. TwoSampleMR in R 4.0.5. Unidirectional only (psychiatric -> AD). No genetic correlation (no LDSC/LAVA). No DLB, no insomnia, no schizophrenia.
- Results: IVs: 14 MDD, 52 bipolar, 22 depression, 13 father severe depression, 24 sibling severe depression, 17 anxiety, 7 autism, 3 PTSD. Table 1 IVW: Depression -> LOAD OR 1.0736 (1.0084–1.1431) P .0264; Depression -> AD 1.0396 (0.9905–1.0911) P .1156; Depression -> EOAD 1.029 (0.907–1.1675); MDD (GERA) -> AD 0.9632 (0.888–1.0447) P .3655; PTSD (3 SNPs) -> AD 0.9975 (0.9734–1.0221) P .8385; bipolar -> AD 1.0674 (0.9875–1.1537) P .1003; anxiety estimates with absurdly wide CIs (e.g. 32.8953 (6e-04–1,685,416)). Heterogeneity for bipolar and anxiety pairs; Egger intercepts all P > .05; MR-PRESSO outliers only anxiety–LOAD; Steiger supports psychiatric -> AD direction. Table 2 confounder-SNP-excluded: Depression -> LOAD 1.0726 (1.0069–1.1425) P .03.
- Discussion: Discrepancy between META depression (positive) and GERA MDD (null) attributed to power/IV differences, not biology. Family-history proxy exposures null, argued to rule out family-history ascertainment artefacts. Specificity to LOAD interpreted via HPA axis, inflammation, neurotrophic mechanisms. Nulls for bipolar, anxiety, PTSD, autism interpreted as observational associations being confounded/reverse causation.
- Limitations stated by authors: sample overlap (FinnGen in both exposure and outcome), mitigated only by F > 10; European ancestry only; inconsistent results across depression datasets / consortia; register-based FinnGen diagnoses (severe cases, heterogeneity); unmeasured pleiotropy cannot be excluded; results preliminary.
- Supplementary material: not available (Tables S1–S8 referenced, not in text file)

## Quality appraisal
Sample size: outcome FinnGen R12 AD 13,964 cases (adequate), but PTSD instrument only 3 SNPs and anxiety instruments give non-informative CIs. Weak MR practice: relaxed IV threshold P < 5 × 10^-6 for most exposures; no multiple-testing correction across ~32 primary tests (single nominal P = .0264 claimed as causal); sample overlap between FinnGen R3-containing depression GWAS and FinnGen R12 outcome not corrected (no MRlap or similar), dismissed via F > 10, which does not remove overlap bias; no reverse-direction MR despite reverse-causation framing; no APOE-specific handling; no genetic correlation; outdated exposure GWAS (not PGC MDD/PTSD/SCZ main releases). Interpretation overstated (Steiger called strong support for unidirectional causality). Venue: Medicine (Baltimore), a high-volume, lower-selectivity journal — flag as low-quality venue. Peer-reviewed. Conflicts with null MDD -> AD MR studies it cites.

## Relevance to this manuscript
D1: recent prior MR of depression/PTSD -> AD with clinical (FinnGen R12) AD outcome; overlaps with our FinnGen sensitivity outcome type (we use R13 clinical AD) but differs in exposures (no PGC MDD2025, no PGC-PTSD Freeze 3, no insomnia, no SCZ, no DLB), methods (no rg/LAVA, no bidirectional MR, no MRlap). Shows that weak, nominal depression -> LOAD signal is fragile and dataset-dependent; PTSD null. D2: explicitly uses clinical rather than family-history-proxy AD. D6: illustrates sample-overlap problem (FinnGen in exposure and outcome) that our MRlap addresses. Does not answer our question with our data.

## Decision
KEEP — recent MR of depression/PTSD on clinically defined FinnGen AD, useful for D1 comparison and as a cautionary example of weak MR practice (cite with caveats).
