## Bibliographic record
Livingston G, Huntley J, Sommerlad A, et al.; 2020; Dementia prevention, intervention, and care: 2020 report of the Lancet Commission; The Lancet 396(10248):413-446; DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(20)30367-6; expert commission report with new systematic reviews/meta-analyses and PAF modelling; access route PMC-XML (PMC7392084)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract (Executive summary/Key messages): 12 potentially modifiable risk factors (adding alcohol, TBI, air pollution to the 2017 nine incl. depression) account for around 40% of dementias; depression might be a risk for dementia but in later life dementia might cause depression; NPS management by multicomponent interventions.
- Introduction: ~50 million with dementia, 152 million by 2050; triangulation framework; most evidence from high-income countries.
- Methods: narrative synthesis of systematic reviews/meta-analyses; new meta-analyses for alcohol (RR 1.18) and TBI (RR 1.84); PAF with communality from >10,000 UK adults; weighted PAF.
- Results: Table 1 relative risks incl. depression (later life) RR 1.9 (1.6-2.3), prevalence 13.2%, weighted PAF 3.9%; overall weighted PAF 39.7%. Depression section: meta-analysis of 32 studies (62,598 participants) pooled effect 2.0 (1.7-2.3); trend to weaker association with longer follow-up; Whitehall late-life symptoms HR 1.7 at 11 years but 1.0 at 22 years; 4922 men HR 1.5 explained by dementia within 5 years; antidepressants do not clearly reduce risk. Sleep: sleep disturbances (incl. insomnia) RR 1.2 for all-cause dementia, 1.6 for AD; U-shaped duration; new-onset late-life sleep disturbance may be part of the dementia natural history; hypnotic findings possibly due to reverse causality and confounding. APOE e4 risk previously estimated at 7% (PAF). NPS: common, clustered, may precede dementia, associated with tau and amyloid. Biomarker section: amyloid positivity rises from 2.7% (50-59 y) to 41.3% (80-89 y).
- Discussion (Strengths and limitations): causal relationship assumed for PAF; LMIC under-represented; risks measured nearer to dementia onset more likely affected by prodromal change; association does not prove causation.
- Limitations stated by authors: assumed causality; global RRs from HIC; communality from a single UK dataset; proxies for social isolation and air pollution; pollution estimate from one study.
- Supplementary material: not available (appendix with PAF formula referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Highly cited Lancet Commission; peer-reviewed; synthesises meta-analyses (depression meta-analysis of 32 studies, 62,598 participants). Observational associations; the authors explicitly flag reverse causation for depression, sleep and physical inactivity. Not MR. Many authors report industry funding outside submitted work. A 2024 update of the Commission exists (not in this batch); for depression/sleep figures this 2020 version remains citable but may be considered superseded for the risk-factor list.

## Relevance to this manuscript
I2: core citation for depression (RR 1.9; pooled 2.0) and sleep disturbance/insomnia (RR 1.2 all-cause, 1.6 AD) as dementia risk factors and, crucially, for the reverse-causation/prodrome problem (Whitehall 22-year attenuation; association confined to first 5 years). I1: NPS common and may precede dementia. D6: lifetime vs late-life exposure timing (risk measured nearer onset more likely prodromal) supports the lifetime vs late-life phenotype limitation.

## Decision
KEEP — authoritative peer-reviewed synthesis giving risk estimates for depression and sleep disturbance with explicit reverse-causation arguments (I2, D6); check whether the 2024 Commission update should be cited alongside.
