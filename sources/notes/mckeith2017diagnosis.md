## Bibliographic record
McKeith IG, Boeve BF, Dickson DW, et al.; 2017; Diagnosis and management of dementia with Lewy bodies: Fourth consensus report of the DLB Consortium; Neurology 89(1):88-100; DOI 10.1212/WNL.0000000000004058; expert consensus diagnostic criteria; access route PMC-XML (PMC5496518)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: revised DLB consensus criteria distinguish clinical features from diagnostic biomarkers; increased weight to RBD and MIBG scintigraphy; pathology criteria updated for AD neuropathologic change; management largely expert opinion; DLB in DSM-5 as major neurocognitive disorder with Lewy bodies.
- Introduction: 2005 criteria increased sensitivity but detection remains suboptimal, many cases missed or misdiagnosed as AD; four working groups plus meeting; prodromal criteria to be reported separately.
- Methods (Summary of changes): core vs supportive clinical features, indicative vs supportive biomarkers; probable/possible DLB; mixed pathology common, up to half of research-diagnosed AD have Lewy-related pathology at autopsy. Note: the body of Table 1 (full criteria list including supportive features) is not present in the text file, only its title.
- Results (criteria content): dementia essential; attention/executive/visuoperceptual deficits typical. Core features: fluctuation; recurrent complex visual hallucinations in up to 80%; parkinsonism eventually >85%; RBD (76% vs 4% in autopsy-confirmed vs non-DLB). Supportive features incl. hypersomnia, hyposmia, severe antipsychotic sensitivity. Indicative biomarkers: reduced DAT uptake (sens 78%, spec 90%), MIBG (69%/87%), PSG REM sleep without atonia (>=90% likelihood of synucleinopathy). Supportive biomarkers: MTL preservation, occipital hypometabolism/cingulate island sign, posterior slow-wave EEG. Amyloid PET positive in >50% of DLB. Genetics: substantial genetic contribution, SNCA variants, GBA mutations overrepresented but most DLB lack them; genetic testing premature. Management: CHEIs; antipsychotic sensitivity; depressive symptoms common but trial data scant.
- Discussion (Future directions): 1-year rule for DLB vs PDD retained; need DLB genetic database, exome repository, family studies; characterise biological effects of APOE, GBA, SNCA and gene-environment interactions.
- Limitations stated by authors: management recommendations largely expert opinion because RCTs in DLB are few; core genes poorly understood; biomarkers indirect; interrater reliability for Lewy subtypes needs work.
- Supplementary material: not available (appendix e-1 and e-references referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Standard international consensus criteria (expert opinion plus evidence review), peer-reviewed, widely used (including by NACC and by DLB GWAS case definitions). Not a primary epidemiological study; percentages cited are from underlying studies. Industry funding of the consortium meeting and many author disclosures. Text file lacks the Table 1 body (supportive clinical features such as depression, anxiety, delusions, hallucinations in other modalities), so those items cannot be quoted from this file.

## Relevance to this manuscript
D4: authoritative source for DLB core clinical features (visual hallucinations up to 80%, fluctuation, parkinsonism, RBD), the frequency of depressive symptoms in DLB, misdiagnosis as AD and mixed AD/Lewy pathology (relevant to clinical phenotype overlap and possible case contamination between AD and DLB GWAS), and DLB genetics (SNCA, GBA, APOE; limited knowledge of core genes; need for larger genetic resources). M1: DLB case definitions in Chia 2021 rely on consensus criteria.

## Decision
KEEP — the reference standard for DLB clinical features and a source for DLB genetics knowledge gaps (D4, D6, M1).
