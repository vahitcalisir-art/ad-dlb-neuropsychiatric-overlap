## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: Insomnia GWAS meta-analysis in 593,724 cases and 1,771,286 controls (almost double the earlier 1.3 million sample); 554 risk loci (364 novel); 3,898 genes naively implicated, 289 prioritized by a new cross-locus strategy; brain-tissue expression specificity and enrichment in synaptic signaling and neuronal differentiation gene sets.
- Introduction: (unheaded main-text opening) insomnia highly prevalent, twin heritability 38–59%, SNP heritability 7% in earlier GWAS; >200 loci in the 1.3 million GWAS with overlap with psychiatric genetic risk; extreme polygenicity; proposes fine-mapping plus cross-locus gene linking; meta-analysis of UK Biobank and 23andMe, 593,724 cases and 1,771,286 controls.
- Methods: UKB GWAS in PLINK 2.0 (logistic regression; age, sex, array, ten PCs; MAC > 100; autosomes, PAR and X); 23andMe summary statistics (logistic regression, five PCs; unrelated Europeans n = 1,978,022); fixed-effect sample-size-weighted METAL meta-analysis; z-to-beta conversion and log OR approximation from case fraction; LDSC SNP heritability with liability-scale conversion using population prevalence 30% (ref. 1, Roth 2007); PRSice PRS in held-out UKB sets with R2 adjusted for ascertainment at prevalence 0.3, plus MVP validation; FUMA loci definition and gene mapping; MAGMA gene, gene-property and gene-set analyses; FINEMAP fine-mapping; coloc eQTL and cross-trait colocalization; InWeb PPI; LDSC genetic correlation with 551 traits (Bonferroni P < 9.07 × 10–5); t-SNE/DBSCAN locus clustering.
- Results: UKB GWAS on 386,988 unrelated European subjects (109,548 cases, 277,440 controls), insomnia from a single dichotomized question validated as a proxy of insomnia disorder; 23andMe GWAS on 1,978,022 unrelated Europeans (484,176 cases, 1,493,846 controls) from multiple online survey questions; per-cohort sample size, prevalence and h2SNP in Supplementary Table 1; cross-cohort rg 0.66; meta-analysis h2SNP 7.2% (s.e.m. 0.17%), LDSC intercept 1.15, ratio 0.079; GWS SNPs explain 17.3% of h2SNP; PRS explained up to 2.46% of variance in UKB holdout and 0.66% in MVP; male–female rg 0.92; 51,876 GWS SNPs in 554 loci (791 lead SNPs); 1,429 MAGMA genes (top PTPRD, LSAMP); significant rg with 350 of 551 traits (cardiovascular, metabolic, psychiatric; individual values only in Supplementary Table 15 / Extended Data Fig. 2); 282 loci colocalized, clustering into metabolic and psychiatric clusters; tissue/cell enrichment (cortex, LGN, habenula, ventral pallidum, anterior pretectal nucleus, GABAergic neurons); 571 HC genes, 289 HCP genes from 239 loci; HCP enrichment in frontal cortex BA9, anterior cingulate BA24 and 18 gene sets.
- Discussion: >2.3 million subjects, 554 loci; metabolic versus psychiatric locus clusters suggest genetic subtypes; habenula, LGN and GABAergic findings discussed; SNP heritability did not increase (7–8%) despite doubled sample, so no gain in prediction (extreme polygenicity, phenotype operationalization); synaptic and neurogenesis gene sets.
- Limitations stated by authors: prioritization strategy feasible only for highly polygenic traits with many loci and single-gene loci; depends on availability and accuracy of functional annotations (may change with more eQTL/rare-variant data); cross-locus linking depends on limited biological networks (PPI); final test of HCP causality requires functional experiments; PRS drop in MVP attributed to phenotype dissimilarity or ancestral mismatch.
- Supplementary material: Supplementary Note and Tables 1–49 (sample sizes, prevalence and h2SNP per cohort in Table 1; rg with 551 traits in Table 15), Supplementary Figs 1–8 and Data not in the supplied text; Extended Data Fig. 1–6 captions read (PRS variance, rg with 350 traits, PRS by metabolic/psychiatric loci, conditional MAGMA analyses, gene-set overlap heatmap). Data availability: full UKB summary statistics and top 10,000 23andMe SNPs public at ctg.cncr.nl; full 23andMe statistics only under agreement with 23andMe.

## Quality appraisal
Large, well-powered GWAS by an experienced group using standard tools (PLINK 2.0, METAL, LDSC, MAGMA, FUMA); European ancestry only. Insomnia in UKB is a single self-report item dichotomized (proxy phenotype), and the 23andMe definition differs (multiple online questions); cross-cohort rg of 0.66 indicates phenotypic heterogeneity between the two components. The UKB-only subset (109,548 cases / 277,440 controls) is the openly downloadable part; case fraction in UKB (~28%) is close to the 30% population prevalence the authors use for liability conversion. Exact UKB-only h2SNP and case prevalence are given only in Supplementary Table 1 (not in the supplied text). Main text gives no individual rg values for depression, PTSD, schizophrenia or Alzheimer's disease.

## Relevance to this manuscript
- Exposure/outcome data: source of the insomnia GWAS summary statistics (UK Biobank-only subset, 109,548 cases and 277,440 controls, European ancestry) used in the study; full meta-analysis (593,724 cases / 1,771,286 controls) requires a 23andMe agreement.
- Population prevalence for liability-scale conversion: the paper itself uses 30% (ref. 1), citable for insomnia prevalence in Steiger/liability-scale analyses.

## Decision
KEEP — source of the insomnia GWAS summary statistics (UK Biobank-only subset) used in the study.
