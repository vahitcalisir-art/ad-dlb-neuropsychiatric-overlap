## Bibliographic record
Trubetskoy V, Pardiñas AF, Qi T, et al. (Schizophrenia Working Group of the Psychiatric Genomics Consortium); 2022; Mapping genomic loci implicates genes and synaptic biology in schizophrenia; Nature 604(7906):502–508; DOI 10.1038/s41586-022-04434-5 (PMID 35396580; PMC9392466; NIH author manuscript); two-stage case–control GWAS meta-analysis with fine-mapping, SMR/eQTL, gene-set and rare-variant convergence analyses; access route PMC-XML (published version, author manuscript)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract (Summary): Heritability 60–80%, much from common alleles; 2-stage GWAS of up to 76,755 people with schizophrenia and 243,649 controls; 287 distinct loci; associations concentrated in genes expressed in CNS neurons (excitatory and inhibitory), not other tissues; 120 prioritised genes (106 protein-coding), 16 with credible non-synonymous/UTR variants; synaptic organisation, differentiation, transmission; convergence with rare-variant genes (GRIN2A, SP4) and neurodevelopmental-disorder genes. (Published Summary uses "2-stage" and "people with schizophrenia" where the earlier text used "two-stage"; British spelling "organisation".)
- Introduction: Schizophrenia typically manifests in late adolescence or early adulthood, with reduced life expectancy, suicide risk and physical illness; risk involves common alleles, rare CNVs and rare coding variants; prior GWAS reported 176 loci; aim is the largest GWAS and prioritisation of variants, genes and processes.
- Methods: Core PGC dataset = 90 EUR and East Asian (ASN) cohorts with individual genotypes: 161,405 unrelated subjects, 67,390 schizophrenia/schizoaffective disorder cases and 94,015 controls (trio = one case + one pseudo-control); ~80% EUR (53,386 cases/77,258 controls), remainder ASN (14,004/16,757); ~half not in the previous PGC GWAS. Primary GWAS also included summary statistics from 9 African-American (6,152/3,918) and Latino (1,234/3,090) cohorts (total 7,386/7,008); primary GWAS 74,776 cases / 101,023 controls; 7,585,078 SNPs MAF ≥ 1% in 175,799 individuals (74.3% EUR, 17.5% ASN, 5.7% AA, 2.5% LAT). Extended GWAS: deCODE (1,979 cases/142,626 EUR controls) for 1,228–1,249 index SNPs P < 1 × 10−5; final 320,404 diploid genomes. Phenotype: schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder (diagnostic details in Supplementary Cohort Descriptions; DSM/ICD not stated in main text). PGC/RICOPILI QC; imputation to HRC r1.1 (EAGLE2/MINIMAC3; trios SHAPEIT3/IMPUTE4); per-cohort additive logistic regression with PCs (PLINK); inverse-variance fixed-effects meta-analysis; index SNPs r2 < 0.1 within 3 Mb; loci = clumps + 50 kb; one SNP kept from extended MHC. Gene boundaries Ensembl 92 (GRCh37) → build GRCh37/hg19. h2SNP in EUR (ref 13; SBayesS per code availability). PRS P+T leave-one-out; liability R2 assuming lifetime risk 1%. Secondary analyses (MAGMA/partitioned LDSC, GO/SynGO, conditional, FINEMAP on 255 regions, SMR/HEIDI with fetal brain, adult brain, blood eQTL; Hi-C) in core dataset only. Summary statistics (Extended, Core, ancestry-specific, sex-stratified) at PGC download site.
- Results: Primary GWAS 313 independent SNPs in 263 loci; Extended GWAS 342 LD-independent SNPs in 287 loci (5 on X); one 2014 association (rs3768644) no longer supported. Male–female rg = 0.992 (SE 0.024). EUR h2SNP = 0.24 (SE 0.007). PRS median liability variance 0.073 (P < 0.05 SNPs), 0.024 for GWS SNPs; median AUC 0.72 in EUR cohorts; top vs bottom PRS centile OR 39 (95% CI 29–53); top centile vs rest OR 5.6 (4.9–6.5). Enrichment in all tested brain regions, excitatory glutamatergic cortical/hippocampal neurons, cortical interneurons, striatal medium spiny neurons; little glial/microglial enrichment (Fig. 4, Extended Data Fig. 3). 24 of 7,315 GO terms, all neuronal; SynGO mainly postsynaptic. FINEMAP: 33 regions with ≤ 5 credible SNPs, 9 single-SNP (rs4766428, ATP2A2); broad fine-map set 628 genes; 70 FINEMAP-prioritised genes; 101 SMR genes, 55 SMR/Hi-C prioritised; 120 prioritised genes (Extended Data Table 1); 15 of 106 SynGO-annotated (Fig. 3). Enrichment for SCHEMA rare-variant genes, ASD and DD genes (Fig. 2); GRIN2A, SP4, STAG1, FAM120A convergent. Numbers identical to the earlier note; wording/format changed (e.g. "SE 0.007" not "s.e. = 0.007").
- Discussion: Largest SCZ GWAS; rare–common convergence implies relevance beyond rare-mutation carriers; neurons are the most important site of pathology; abnormal neuronal function not confined to a few brain structures; synaptic organisation/differentiation/transmission; prioritised genes as resource. No genetic correlations with other traits in the main text (only male–female rg).
- Limitations stated by authors: no dedicated section; PRS liability explained insufficient to predict diagnosis in the general population; PRS performs best in EUR given ancestry composition; secondary analyses restricted to core dataset with individual-level data; fine-mapping excluded MHC; synapse-type specificity unresolved.
- Supplementary material: not available (Supplementary Tables 1–28 and Note referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Very large, well-powered consortium GWAS, peer-reviewed (Nature). Uniform QC/imputation for the core dataset; independent deCODE replication en masse. Ancestry predominantly European (74.3% of primary GWAS; deCODE EUR only). Trio and case–control designs mixed. Sample overlap with other PGC GWAS possible (shared controls such as WTCCC acknowledged), relevant for MR/MRlap. No UK Biobank cases mentioned. Liability scaling uses 1% lifetime risk. Many authors declare industry ties (listed). Not an MR study.

## Relevance to this manuscript
- M1: primary description of the SCZ GWAS (case/control counts, core vs extended, EUR/ASN/AA/LAT breakdown, schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder, HRC imputation, GRCh37, public summary statistics including ancestry-specific files — our analyses should specify the EUR or core file; the Extended set includes deCODE).
- M3: source for SCZ lifetime risk 1% used for liability-scale conversion (Extended Data Fig. 2 caption).
- D5: neuronal and synaptic enrichment; fine-mapped genes (GRIN2A, SP4, CACNA1C, ATP2A2); little glial/microglial enrichment (contrast with AD microglial genetics).
- D6: mostly EUR ancestry; limited PRS prediction; early-adulthood onset/lifetime phenotype vs late-life dementia outcomes.

## Decision
KEEP — the schizophrenia GWAS data source actually used (PGC3), peer-reviewed; primary citation for SCZ sample description and 1% lifetime risk.
