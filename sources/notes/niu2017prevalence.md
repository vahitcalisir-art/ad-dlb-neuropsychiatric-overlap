## Bibliographic record
Niu H, Álvarez-Álvarez I, Guillén-Grima F, Aguinaga-Ontoso I; 2017; Prevalence and incidence of Alzheimer's disease in Europe: a meta-analysis; Neurología (English Edition) 32(8):523–532; DOI 10.1016/j.nrleng.2016.02.009 (Spanish original DOI 10.1016/j.nrl.2016.02.016); systematic review and Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis of observational studies; access route: open access full text (published English edition) on elsevier.es, retrieved 2026-11-21 as HTML

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: Meta-analysis of AD prevalence and incidence in Europe (Medline, Scopus, CINAHL); Bayesian random-effects model with 95% credible intervals; prevalence 5.05% (4.73–5.39), men 3.31%, women 7.13%, increasing with age; incidence 11.08 per 1000 person-years.
- Introduction: AD 50–75% of dementia; risk factors (sex, age, education, APOE ε4, smoking, obesity, diabetes); earlier EURODEM prevalence 4.4% above age 65; projected growth of case numbers.
- Methods: articles 1995–2015 in five languages; inclusion: original observational studies of European populations, participants older than 50, original prevalence/incidence data, described AD diagnosis, full text; two extractors with arbitration; Fast*Pro Bayesian random-effects model; leave-one-out sensitivity; I² heterogeneity.
- Results: 18 articles (8 cross-sectional for prevalence, 11 longitudinal for incidence; one in both); countries Spain, Netherlands, Italy, France, Greece, UK, Sweden, Denmark; N 288–7528. Prevalence 5.05% (4.73–5.39); 4.66% after excluding one study; southern Europe 6.88%, northern 4.31%; by age 0.97% (65–74), 7.66% (75–84), 22.53% (≥85); I² 42.42% (6.49% after exclusion). Incidence 11.08 per 1000 person-years, higher in women and with age; high heterogeneity (I² 70.37%).
- Discussion: comparison with EURODEM, UK decline of dementia prevalence, Asian and US estimates; lifestyle explanations offered without data; claims novelty.
- Limitations stated by authors: limited databases, so possibly incomplete study capture.
- Supplementary material: none referenced.

## Quality appraisal
Small meta-analysis (8 prevalence studies) restricted to older European populations, moderate heterogeneity, one database-limited search; prevalence refers to older adults (study age ranges mostly ≥65), not to the whole adult population. Adequate as a cited, Europe-specific AD prevalence for liability-scale conversion in Steiger filtering, where the primary AD GWAS consists of older European cases and controls; the uncertainty is covered by the pre-specified 0.5×–2× sensitivity range.
