## Bibliographic record
Fresnais D, Fure B; 2026; Anxiety Symptoms and Risk of Dementia Progression in Mild Cognitive Impairment: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis; Dementia and Geriatric Cognitive Disorders; DOI 10.1159/dem/aciag007; systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of cohort studies (PRISMA, GRADE); access route PMC-XML (PMC13695522)

## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: 15 cohort studies (14 meta-analysed) on anxiety and MCI-to-dementia conversion; anxiety associated with all-cause dementia HR 1.25 (95% CI 1.11-1.41) and AD HR 1.32 (1.16-1.49); pooled OR not significant (1.15; 0.84-1.57).
- Introduction: NPS almost invariably seen over the course of dementia and may precede cognitive symptoms (BPSD vs MBI); anxiety understudied relative to depression/apathy; earlier 2018 meta-analysis; conflicting newer studies.
- Methods: Medline, EMBASE, Cochrane, PsycINFO, CINAHL, Epistemonikos (Sept 2025, from 2000, English/Scandinavian); PECOS; two investigators; adjusted HRs pooled with inverse-variance random effects in RevMan 5.4; OR by Mantel-Haenszel; SMD; leave-one-out; CASP quality; GRADE certainty; funnel plot.
- Results: 15 studies 2007-2024 (sample sizes 47-2250 MCI), anxiety prevalence 4.8%-54%, conversion 10.5%-51.1%, follow-up 1.2-5.4 years. Pooled HR all-cause 1.25 (1.11-1.41), losing significance after removing Rosenberg 2013 (HR 1.22; 0.98-1.51; p = 0.08); AD HR 1.32 (1.16-1.49), still significant after exclusion (HR 1.55; 1.12-2.13). OR 1.15 (0.84-1.57); SMD 0.16 (-0.03 to 0.36). GRADE very low.
- Discussion: HR vs OR discrepancy explained by time-to-event analyses; anxiety may reflect disease awareness, prodromal manifestation, or causal pathway (HPA axis, vascular); reverse causation discussed; depression confounding unlikely sole explanation; twin study suggests shared genetic factors partially explain anxiety-dementia association; clinical utility uncertain.
- Limitations stated by authors: HRs not available from all studies; variable adjustment sets; baseline-only anxiety (misclassification); attrition; few studies in subgroups; primary result not robust to leave-one-out; heterogeneous anxiety definitions.
- Supplementary material: not available (search strategy and PRISMA checklist referenced only)

## Quality appraisal
Small meta-analysis (8 studies in primary HR analysis), mainly memory-clinic MCI samples; GRADE very low certainty; primary all-cause result not robust. Peer-reviewed. Not MR; observational association with acknowledged reverse-causation and confounding concerns. No conflicts of interest.

## Relevance to this manuscript
Anxiety is not one of the manuscript's exposures (MDD, PTSD, insomnia, SCZ). Marginal support for I2 (reverse causation/prodromal interpretation of NPS and mention that shared genetic factors partly explain a psychiatric-dementia association), but the prodromal argument is covered better in this batch by sassi2026mild (MBI meta-analysis) and the risk-factor framing by livingston2020dementia.

## Decision
DROP — off-target exposure (anxiety in MCI), very low certainty, primary estimate not robust; the reverse-causation point is better supported by sassi2026mild.
