## Section-by-section reading log
- Abstract: IVW-MR with GWAS summary statistics is subject to weak-instrument bias and winner's curse, both modified by exposure–outcome sample overlap. MRlap simultaneously considers weak-instrument bias and winner's curse while accounting for potential overlap; assumes spike-and-slab architecture and uses LD score regression to derive, estimate and correct the IVW bias from summary statistics only. Simulations: bias reduced in all scenarios, up to 30-fold; bias decreases with sample size and is larger for low-heritability/high-polygenicity exposures. UKBB obesity-related exposures: significant IVW vs corrected differences for both nonoverlapping and fully overlapping samples; method allows use of larger, overlapping GWAS samples.
- Introduction: MR assumptions (relevance, exchangeability, exclusion restriction; Figure 1). Overlap modifies weak-instrument bias and winner's curse: nonoverlapping two-sample estimates biased toward the null; overlapping estimates biased toward the observational correlation (incl. confounding). NOME/regression dilution bias proportional to 1/F; SIMEX, radial MR, MR-RAPS, dIVW; winner's curse causes underestimation in nonoverlapping and bias toward observational correlation in fully overlapping samples; third-sample selection and single-SNP corrections (Palmer & Pe'er; Zhong & Prentice) do not model genome-wide IV selection. Uncorrelated pleiotropy (InSIDE) leaves IVW consistent; correlated pleiotropy not explicitly tackled by MRlap, so compared against IVW, MR-RAPS, dIVW, weighted median, weighted mode.
- Methods: Model with standardized traits/genotypes (mean 0, variance 1), single environmental confounder (rho = kx*ky). IVW expectation derived with selection threshold T on |beta_x|*sqrt(nA) (e.g. T≈5.45 for p=5e-8), giving E[IVW] as function of alpha, nA, nB, T, polygenicity pi_x, per-variant heritability sigma_x^2 and lambda' (cross-trait LDSC intercept; only term affected by overlap). Without overlap the bias is multiplicative toward the null; with overlap direction depends on rho. Corrected estimator (eq. 12), its SE and covariance with IVW (Suppl. SC); Burgess 2016 formula is a special case under the null without winner's curse. Implemented in R package MRlap (v0.0.2) using TwoSampleMR functions and GenomicSEM LDSC; 1000G LD scores; IVs selected at several T thresholds and made independent by 500 kb distance pruning. Simulations on UKBB genotypes (379,530 unrelated British, ~1.15M HapMap3 SNPs), 100 datasets per setting, overlap 0–100% in 25% steps; standard settings nA=nB=20,000, pi_x=0.001, h2=0.4, kx=0.3, ky=0.5; scenarios: negative/weaker/stronger confounder, binary exposure (prevalence 0.1), realistic parameters, full vs split UKBB, uncorrelated and correlated pleiotropy; RMSE, coverage, CI width, power, between/within-group variance, t_diff test (eq. 17); comparison with weighted median, weighted mode, MR-RAPS, dIVW at p<5e-8. UKBB application: 100,000-individual samples, inverse-normal transformed phenotypes adjusted for sex, age, age^2, 40 PCs; BMI→BMI, SBP→SBP (nonoverlapping only), BMI→SBP/smoking/alcohol across overlaps; Steiger filter.
- Results: Standard settings: 10% overestimation (full overlap) and 15% underestimation (no overlap) by IVW; between/within-group variance ratio up to 26× larger for IVW; corrected RMSE stable across overlaps, coverage ~95%; corrected absolute bias <0.012 vs up to 0.033. Negative confounder: IVW underestimates for all overlaps (13% average). Null effect: IVW unbiased without overlap but biased toward confounder-induced correlation with full overlap; FPR 20–40% vs much better control after correction. Binary exposure similar (2.3× bias). Realistic parameters: 22% under/30% overestimation, corrected five times less biased. Full-sample MRlap vs split-sample IVW: IVW 3.27× more biased, MRlap 3.43× lower variance, coverage 88% vs 72%, power 100% vs 94%. Correlated pleiotropy biases both IVW and MRlap; weighted median/mode/MR-RAPS biased toward observational correlation at ≥50% overlap; MR-RAPS and dIVW performed poorly. UKBB: same-trait IVW biased toward null (coverage 0–18%), corrected not different from 1; BMI→SBP nonoverlap IVW 0.095 vs corrected 0.126 (pdiff=.0049); BMI→smoking 0.130 vs 0.170 (pdiff=.0137, max overlap 48.5%); BMI→alcohol corrected stronger (−0.244 to −0.2515) suggesting a concordant confounder. Figure captions 2–6 read (simulation panels a–d; method comparison; same-trait; BMI→SBP/alcohol).
- Discussion: MRlap models the full IV selection process, overlap and exposure estimation error. Bias direction/magnitude depends on overlap and confounder sign; elevated Type I error with overlap under the null. Correction increases variance but reduces RMSE in at least one overlap degree in every scenario. Thresholds limited to <1e-6. Threshold-dependent heterogeneity may reflect phenotypic heterogeneity or genetic confounding (CAUSE, LHC-MR, MR-Clust needed). Recommends MRlap as a sensitivity analysis: if corrected and IVW do not differ significantly, IVW can be used (lower variance); otherwise corrected effects preferred. Internal inconsistency persists in the published version: Results report 10% over (full overlap)/15% under (no overlap) for standard settings, Discussion reports 15% over/10% under. Data availability: data available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request; no conflict of interest; open access funded by Université de Lausanne.
- Limitations stated by authors: biased under a genetic confounder/correlated pleiotropy; relies on spike-and-slab architecture (deviations reduce efficiency); developed for continuous traits—valid for case–control designs only if overlap degree does not differ between cases and controls; single-confounder simulation simplification (bias estimation does not depend on it); local LD in IV selection not modelled (small residual winner's curse).
- Supplementary material: not available (Supporting Information Sections SA–SE, Figures S1–S15, Tables S1–S15 referenced; not read)

## Quality appraisal
Peer-reviewed published version read in full. Rigorous analytic derivation with realistic UKBB-based simulations and empirical application; open-source R implementation; no conflict of interest; data only on request. Minor internal inconsistency in reported standard-setting bias percentages (Results vs Discussion). Key caveat for this manuscript: our outcomes (AD, DLB) and several exposures are case–control binary traits; MRlap validity for case–control requires similar overlap in cases and controls. The model is derived for standardized variables (mean 0, variance 1), and IV selection (threshold T, distance pruning) is part of the method; the paper does not explicitly state that the package output is on a standardized-beta scale. Consistent with burgess2016bias (special case).

## Relevance to this manuscript
M2: citation for MRlap method (joint correction of weak-instrument bias, winner's curse and sample overlap using cross-trait LDSC; R package built on TwoSampleMR and GenomicSEM LDSC; own IV selection by threshold and pruning; standardized-variable model) and its interpretation as a sensitivity analysis (corrected vs IVW). D6: limitations—case–control applicability, correlated pleiotropy not corrected, overlap bias directions.
