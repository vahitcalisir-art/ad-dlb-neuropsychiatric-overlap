# Introduction

Most people with dementia develop neuropsychiatric symptoms at some stage, although individual symptoms differ widely in prevalence and persistence [@zhu2026neuropsychiatric#c1] <!--id:intro_nps-->. In Alzheimer's disease (AD), about 85% of patients develop such a symptom during the illness [@kumar2026genetic#c7]. A longitudinal clinical cohort recorded depressed mood in 51% of participants with AD and in 65.2% of those with clinically diagnosed dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), in whom psychosis was also frequent [@zhu2026neuropsychiatric#c2; @zhu2026neuropsychiatric#c3; @zhu2026neuropsychiatric#c7]. Psychosis in DLB was associated with faster functional decline in that cohort [@zhu2026neuropsychiatric#c6], and depressive symptoms in DLB are common although trial data on their treatment are scant [@mckeith2017diagnosis#c2]. Mild behavioral impairment, defined by later-life onset and at least six months of persistence, carried a pooled hazard ratio of 2.70 for incident dementia [@sassi2026mild#c4; @sassi2026mild#c1]. The pooled studies were highly heterogeneous, and the authors suggested that mild behavioral impairment may mark several dementia subtypes besides AD [@sassi2026mild#c2; @sassi2026mild#c6].

Psychiatric disorders that begin earlier in life are also associated with later dementia, and the 2024 Lancet Commission found a higher risk of dementia 10 to 14 years after a diagnosis of depression [@livingston2024dementia#c8; @livingston2024dementia#c4] <!--id:intro_psych-->. The Commission concluded that depression increases risk at all adult ages, although part of the late-life association reflects preclinical dementia [@livingston2024dementia#c1]. For post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), a meta-analysis suggested a higher risk with considerable heterogeneity between studies [@livingston2024dementia#c6], which led the Commission to judge the evidence too heterogeneous to classify PTSD as a modifiable risk factor [@livingston2024dementia#c5]. People with schizophrenia (SCZ) develop dementia more often and earlier than others, yet it is unclear whether SCZ predisposes to dementia independently of the risk factors that accompany it [@livingston2024dementia#c8; @livingston2024dementia#c9]. A meta-analysis reported higher risks of all-cause dementia and AD with insomnia [@meng2025insomnia#c1], whereas a Norwegian cohort found no association with either [@livingston2024dementia#c7]. The meta-analytic association was stronger when follow-up lasted less than five years [@meng2025insomnia#c2], a pattern expected if risks identified in the few years before dementia reflect reverse causation as well as, or instead of, a causal effect [@livingston2024dementia#c2] <!--id:intro_revcaus-->. Neuropsychiatric symptoms often appear years before measurable cognitive impairment [@sassi2026mild#c3], and insomnia and dementia are thought to influence each other in both directions [@meng2025insomnia#c4].

Genome-wide genetic correlation (rg), estimated by linkage disequilibrium (LD) score regression (LDSC), requires only genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics and is not biased by sample overlap [@buliksullivan2015atlas#c1]. Mendelian randomization (MR) uses genetic variants as instruments, so its estimates reflect the lifetime effect of genetic liability to the exposure [@skrivankova2021strengthening2#c7; @skrivankova2021strengthening2#c8], and applying it in both directions addresses the direction of a causal association [@skrivankova2021strengthening2#c4]. Earlier genome-wide analyses found no genetic correlation between AD and major depressive disorder (MDD) [@gibson2017assessing#c1; @monereosanchez2021genetic#c1] <!--id:intro_null-->. An early atlas of genetic correlations concluded that the genetic basis of AD is distinct from psychiatric conditions [@buliksullivan2015atlas#c8], and a recent study reported near-zero correlations of AD with depression and SCZ [@kumar2026genetic#c2]. MR has likewise found no causal effect of sleep traits, including insomnia, or of MDD on AD [@huang2020sleep#c2; @huang2020sleep#c9]. Genetic liability to AD was associated with a lower risk of insomnia [@huang2020sleep#c1], and in the same study the reverse-direction associations with sleep duration depended on the APOE ε4 allele [@huang2020sleep#c7].

A null genome-wide result does not exclude shared genetic effects, since local correlations of opposite sign can cancel out in a genome-wide estimate [@werme2022integrated#c1] <!--id:intro_local-->. Bivariate mixture modelling found that a large share of the causal variants for AD overlapped with depression despite a negligible genome-wide correlation [@monereosanchez2021genetic#c4; @monereosanchez2021genetic#c5]. Among psychiatric traits, local correlations reveal more heterogeneity than genome-wide estimates [@nievergelt2024genome#c8]. Using the Local Analysis of [co]Variant Annotation (LAVA) method, one study reported 17 loci shared between AD and depression and 23 between AD and SCZ [@kumar2026genetic#c3], at an uncorrected threshold of P < 5 × 10⁻³ [@kumar2026genetic#c5]. Used as instruments for AD, APOE variants violate the exclusion restriction because of their pleiotropy [@huang2020sleep#c6]. APOE ε4 is also the major common genetic risk factor for DLB, and whether it acts mainly through coexisting AD pathology is unresolved [@watkins2026understanding#c2; @watkins2026understanding#c5]. The largest DLB GWAS included 2,591 cases, compared with more than 60,000 in recent AD GWAS [@watkins2026understanding#c1], and had limited power for common variants of small effect [@chia2021genome#c8] <!--id:intro_dlb-->. DLB is often missed or misdiagnosed as AD, and mixed AD and Lewy-related pathology is common [@mckeith2017diagnosis#c3; @mckeith2017diagnosis#c4]. The largest AD GWAS, in turn, combines clinically diagnosed cases with proxy cases defined by parental history [@bellenguez2022new#c4; @bellenguez2022new#c2], and proxy-based AD GWAS can give misleading genetic correlations [@wu2024pervasive#c1]. In proxy GWAS of AD based on parental history, the component unrelated to AD correlated negatively with MDD and SCZ [@wu2024pervasive#c4], and survival bias is expected to produce spurious negative correlations with other health outcomes [@wu2024pervasive#c5]. FinnGen, by contrast, defines AD from register-based diagnosis codes and is enriched for AD cases [@kurki2023finngen#c4; @kurki2023finngen#c7].

The present study examined MDD, PTSD, insomnia and SCZ against AD and DLB with genome-wide rg [@buliksullivan2015atlas#c1], local rg with LAVA [@werme2022integrated#c1] and bidirectional two-sample MR [@hemani2018mr#c1], using APOE-excluded analyses and a register-based AD GWAS without proxy cases (FinnGen) as sensitivity analyses [@huang2020sleep#c6; @wu2024pervasive#c6] <!--id:intro_aim-->.
