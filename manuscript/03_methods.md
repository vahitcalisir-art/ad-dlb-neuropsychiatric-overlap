# Materials and methods

## Study design and analysis plan

Four psychiatric traits (major depressive disorder, MDD; post-traumatic stress disorder, PTSD; insomnia; schizophrenia, SCZ) were examined against Alzheimer's disease (AD) and dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) with genome-wide genetic correlation, local genetic correlation and bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR). The eight psychiatric × dementia combinations formed the primary pairs. AD × DLB and the six pairs among the psychiatric traits were secondary pairs, reported descriptively and corrected in their own family. Figure 1 summarizes the analysis layers and data sources.

{{figure:Figure1_design.png|Study design. Genome-wide genetic correlation, local genetic correlation and bidirectional MR were applied to four psychiatric traits and two dementias; colocalization was conditional on local results, and FinnGen AD served as a sensitivity analysis without UK Biobank or proxy cases. Solid boxes denote primary analyses and dashed boxes sensitivity analyses.}}

Hypotheses, thresholds, multiple-testing families and an interpretation rule for MR were fixed in an analysis plan, frozen with a SHA-256 checksum before cross-trait analyses, when only single-file harmonization and quality control had been run. The plan was not deposited in a public registry. Supplementary Table S1 lists every later change with its reason and timing. Reporting follows STROBE-MR [@skrivankova2021strengthening2#c1], and the checklist is completed item by item in Supplementary File S1.

## Data sources

All analyses used publicly available GWAS summary statistics of European ancestry (Table 1). No individual-level data were accessed, and no dataset requiring an application, a data-use agreement or a fee was used.

{{table:table1_data_sources.csv|Summary statistics used in the study. Counts are those declared by each release; "Shared cohorts" lists cohorts contributing to more than one of the GWAS used here.}}

The primary AD GWAS combined clinically diagnosed cases with UK Biobank proxy cases defined by parental history of dementia [@bellenguez2022new#c1; @bellenguez2022new#c2]. Because its UK Biobank component also contributed controls, it overlaps with every other GWAS that includes UK Biobank [@bellenguez2022new#c5]. DLB summary statistics came from a GWAS of autopsy-confirmed and clinically probable cases [@chia2021genome#c1; @chia2021genome#c3]. The primary MDD file was the Psychiatric Genomics Consortium release excluding UK Biobank and 23andMe [@adams2025trans#c1; @adams2025trans#c7], chosen to remove AD × MDD overlap by design, and the release including UK Biobank served as a sensitivity analysis. PTSD summary statistics came from the Psychiatric Genomics Consortium Freeze 3 European-ancestry meta-analysis, whose electronic health record cohorts include UK Biobank and FinnGen [@nievergelt2024genome#c1; @nievergelt2024genome#c3]. LDSC and LAVA need only Z scores and sample sizes, so the sample-size-weighted Z-score file was used for them [@buliksullivan2015atlas#c1; @werme2022integrated#c10], while MR used the case/control file with effect sizes from the same release.

Insomnia summary statistics were the openly available UK Biobank-only subset of the insomnia GWAS, with 109,548 cases and 277,440 controls [@watanabe2022genome#c1]; the full meta-analysis including 23andMe requires a data transfer agreement and was not used. SCZ summary statistics came from the European-ancestry autosomes of the third Psychiatric Genomics Consortium SCZ GWAS [@trubetskoy2022mapping#c1]. The sensitivity analysis required an AD GWAS free of the proxy cases and UK Biobank samples of the primary AD GWAS [@bellenguez2022new#c2]. The FinnGen release 13 AD endpoint met this requirement as a register-based AD GWAS without proxy cases, defined from register-based diagnosis codes in hospital discharge and cause-of-death registers [@kurki2023finngen#c1; @kurki2023finngen#c4]. A larger clinically diagnosed AD GWAS was available only under data-use conditions that could not be reconciled with the analysis of psychiatric traits, so it was not used.

## Harmonization and quality control

Each file was harmonized separately to GRCh37, and files on GRCh38 were mapped by rsID to the 1000 Genomes Phase 3 European reference [@auton2015global#c1; @auton2015global#c2]. The following thresholds were analysis-plan choices. SNPs with minor allele frequency below 0.01, imputation INFO below 0.9 where reported, multi-allelic or duplicated positions, and strand-ambiguous palindromic SNPs with minor allele frequency above 0.42 were removed. Genome-wide and local genetic correlation analyses excluded the major histocompatibility complex region (chr6:25–34 Mb), and the APOE region (chr19:44.4–46.5 Mb) was flagged for the APOE-excluded analyses. For every file, allele-frequency concordance with the reference, genomic inflation and declared sample sizes were checked; Supplementary Table S2 gives the number of SNPs remaining after each step.

## Genome-wide genetic correlation

SNP heritability and genetic correlation (rg) were estimated by LD score regression [@buliksullivan2015ld#c1; @buliksullivan2015atlas#c1] as implemented in GenomicSEM [@grotzinger2019genomic#c1], with HapMap3 SNPs and LD scores from the 1000 Genomes European panel [@buliksullivan2015atlas#c6]. Case/control traits were entered with their effective sample size and a sample prevalence of 0.5, which places heritability on the observed scale. Sample overlap does not bias cross-trait LDSC, because it is absorbed by the cross-trait intercept [@buliksullivan2015atlas#c2; @grotzinger2019genomic#c2]. That intercept was reported for every pair as an empirical index of overlap. Genetic correlations of traits with a heritability Z score below 4 are generally too noisy to report [@buliksullivan2015atlas#c9], and the analysis plan flagged such traits as low power while still reporting their estimates. The plan specified three sensitivity analyses: the MDD file including UK Biobank [@adams2025trans#c1], the PTSD case/control file [@nievergelt2024genome#c1] and AD with the APOE region removed.

## Local genetic correlation

Local genetic correlation was estimated with LAVA version 0.1.5 [@werme2022integrated#c4] across 2,495 approximately independent genomic loci, excluding loci that overlap the major histocompatibility complex. The analysis plan specified the UK Biobank LD reference as primary, following the LAVA developers' recommendation for European-ancestry data [@werme2025lava#c1; @werme2025lava#c3]. The developers attribute possible type 1 error inflation and local heritability bias to the smaller 1000 Genomes panel [@werme2025lava#c2], which was analysed as a sensitivity analysis [@auton2015global#c2]. LDSC cross-trait intercepts supplied the sample overlap model [@werme2022integrated#c5; @werme2022integrated#c6], and binary traits were entered with case and control counts derived from the effective sample size (Supplementary Table S3).

Local heritability was first tested for each trait at each locus. A locus was carried forward for a pair only if both traits had a univariate P below 1 × 10⁻⁴, since local correlations cannot be interpreted without local genetic signal in both traits [@werme2022integrated#c4]. LAVA then estimated bivariate local rho with its 95% CI for every carried-forward locus–pair [@werme2022integrated#c6]. Locus–pairs with uncorrected P < 0.05 that did not pass FDR correction are described as nominal and treated as exploratory. Because LAVA obtains bivariate P values and confidence intervals by simulation [@werme2022integrated#c9], each locus–pair was analysed with its own fixed random seed derived from a single base seed, so that every estimate can be reproduced. Loci in the APOE region were identified separately. For the 1000 Genomes analysis, palindromic SNPs whose allele frequency fell on the opposite side of 0.5 from the reference were removed as possible strand mismatches [@auton2015global#c2], and all locus–pairs that passed FDR correction in the main analysis were re-estimated after the same exclusion.

## Mendelian randomization

Sixteen primary MR analyses were run, one for each direction of the eight primary pairs. Each analysis relied on the three core instrumental variable assumptions: the instruments are associated with the exposure, share no unmeasured cause with the outcome and affect the outcome only through the exposure [@skrivankova2021strengthening2#c9]. Instruments were SNPs associated with the exposure at P < 5 × 10⁻⁸, with a plan-specified fallback of P < 5 × 10⁻⁶ when fewer than three instruments remained. Clumping in PLINK 1.9 [@chang2015second#c1; @chang2015second#c4] against the 1000 Genomes European reference kept only approximately independent SNPs [@hemani2018mr#c3]; the clumping window (r² < 0.001 within 10,000 kb) was an analysis-plan choice. SNPs with an F statistic of 10 or less were removed, a plan threshold motivated by the smaller weak-instrument and overlap bias of strong instruments [@hemani2018mr#c7]. Instruments absent from the outcome GWAS were dropped without LD proxies, and exposure and outcome data were harmonized with TwoSampleMR [@hemani2018mr#c1]. Palindromic SNPs were resolved by allele frequency, except for insomnia, whose file reports no allele frequency, so all its palindromic SNPs were removed.

Steiger filtering removed SNPs explaining more variance in the outcome than in the exposure [@hemani2018mr#c5; @hemani2017orienting#c8]. For binary traits this variance was computed on the liability scale, which requires population prevalences [@hemani2017orienting#c6]. The values were 0.15 for MDD [@adams2025trans#c5], 0.01 for SCZ [@trubetskoy2022mapping#c6], 0.001 for DLB [@chia2021genome#c6], 0.05 for PTSD [@koenen2017posttraumatic#c3] and 0.05 for AD (European prevalence among people aged over 50) [@niu2017prevalence#c1; @niu2017prevalence#c2]. The value for insomnia (0.10) was a working assumption. Filtering was repeated with every prevalence halved and doubled, recording the instruments removed and the IVW estimate on each retained set. Because the insomnia GWAS authors used a population prevalence of 30% [@watanabe2022genome#c6], which lies outside this range, filtering in the four insomnia directions was also repeated with 0.30. Genome-wide and local genetic correlations do not depend on these values, and MR estimates depend on them only through the retained instruments.

The primary estimator was the multiplicative random-effects inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method [@hemani2018mr#c2]. Sensitivity estimators were MR-Egger [@bowden2015mendelian#c1; @bowden2015mendelian#c3], the weighted median [@bowden2016consistent#c1], the weighted mode [@hartwig2017robust#c1] and MR-PRESSO with its global, outlier and distortion tests [@verbanck2018detection#c1; @verbanck2018detection#c2; @verbanck2018detection#c3], together with leave-one-out analysis [@hemani2018mr#c4]. MR-Egger assumes that instrument strength is independent of direct pleiotropic effects (InSIDE) [@bowden2015mendelian#c2], and the weighted median requires at least half of the weight to come from valid instruments [@bowden2016consistent#c2]. The weighted mode requires that the largest weights among subsets of instruments with similar estimates come from valid instruments [@hartwig2017robust#c3]. Heterogeneity was assessed with Cochran's Q for the IVW model and Rücker's Q′ for MR-Egger [@bowden2015mendelian#c7], and the I²GX statistic indexed regression dilution of MR-Egger [@bowden2016assessing#c2]. When I²GX was below 0.9, MR-Egger was re-estimated with simulation extrapolation (SIMEX), and the SIMEX intercept entered the interpretation rule [@bowden2016assessing#c5; @bowden2016assessing#c6]. MR-PRESSO and SIMEX used fixed seeds [@verbanck2018detection#c1; @bowden2016assessing#c6].

Sample overlap biases two-sample MR estimates toward the observational association [@mounier2023bias#c3], so MRlap was applied to every primary direction and to the sensitivity analyses that used MDD with UK Biobank [@mounier2023bias#c1; @mounier2023bias#c2]. Because MRlap selects its own instruments [@mounier2023bias#c10] and its model is formulated for standardized variables [@mounier2023bias#c9], its observed and corrected estimates are reported as standardized betas alongside the IVW odds ratio. Every direction with AD as exposure or outcome was repeated after removing instruments in the APOE region. Effects are expressed as the odds ratio of the outcome per unit increase in the log odds of genetic liability to the exposure. As context for null results, the minimum detectable OR at 80% power and two-sided α of 0.05 was computed from instrument R² and the outcome sample.

The interpretation rule was fixed in the analysis plan. A finding was described as consistent evidence only if three conditions held. First, the IVW estimate passed FDR correction across the 16 primary tests. Second, at least two of MR-Egger, the weighted median, the weighted mode and the MR-PRESSO outlier-corrected estimate agreed in direction with IVW [@bowden2016consistent#c8; @hartwig2017robust#c8; @verbanck2018detection#c2]. Third, the MR-Egger intercept, conventional or SIMEX as appropriate, had P > 0.05 [@bowden2015mendelian#c3]. A finding that met the first condition only was described as evidence sensitive to pleiotropy, and any other result as no evidence. Where overlap could not be removed by design, a finding was further qualified if the MRlap-corrected estimate changed direction or lost nominal significance [@mounier2023bias#c6].

## Sensitivity analysis with FinnGen

FinnGen AD was analysed against the four psychiatric traits for genome-wide rg and for MR in both directions, with the same instrument rules, estimators, MRlap and APOE-excluded analyses [@kurki2023finngen#c4]. Because FinnGen contributes to the MDD and PTSD GWAS [@nievergelt2024genome#c3], MRlap was applied to every FinnGen direction. FinnGen draws on a founder population whose LD structure differs from the European reference panels used here [@kurki2023finngen#c3; @auton2015global#c5]. These analyses served as consistency checks and were not used to declare significance; for genome-wide rg, correction across the four FinnGen tests is shown [@kurki2023finngen#c1].

## Colocalization

Colocalization with coloc [@giambartolomei2014bayesian#c1] was planned for each primary locus–pair significant at FDR 5% in the main LAVA analysis, within LAVA locus boundaries and with default priors. A posterior probability above 0.8 for a shared causal variant was to be interpreted as colocalization [@giambartolomei2014bayesian#c3].

## Multiple testing

The Benjamini–Hochberg procedure [@benjamini1995controlling#c1] controlled the FDR at 5% within each family defined in the analysis plan, namely the 8 primary genome-wide rg tests, all bivariate LAVA tests of the primary pairs and the 16 primary IVW tests. Bonferroni-corrected values were also reported. Secondary pairs formed separate families, and sensitivity analyses were not used to declare significance.

## Software and reproducibility

Analyses used R 4.3.3 with GenomicSEM 0.0.5 [@grotzinger2019genomic#c1], LAVA 0.1.5 [@werme2025lava#c3], TwoSampleMR 0.7.14 [@hemani2018mr#c1], MRPRESSO 1.0 [@verbanck2018detection#c1], MRlap 0.0.3.3 [@mounier2023bias#c1], simex 1.8, coloc 5.2.3 [@giambartolomei2014bayesian#c1] and PLINK 1.9 [@chang2015second#c1]. All scripts used a fixed base seed. The complete pipeline was re-run from the raw summary statistics in a separate directory and reproduced all reported estimates, tables and figures; only file paths, run times and timestamps differed. Departures from the analysis plan comprised data-processing corrections, an implementation detail of the input for binary traits and the replacement of two prevalence values by cited ones. They also included an additional descriptive power measure, an additional descriptive check of the insomnia prevalence and the fixed seeds for the local genetic correlation analysis. Supplementary Table S1 describes each with its timing.
